# Reading pack (Lab path, Lessons 3B.2a–b)

Five **real** published papers on future thinking, given to the lab agent for Dr. Lund's lit review. Each file holds the paper's title, authors, journal, DOI, and **abstract only**. Abstracts keep the pack small enough for the free plan, and they also make a point: **an abstract isn't the paper.** Before citing a finding for real, read the full article (every DOI link is free to read).

| File | Paper |
|---|---|
| `bromberg-2017.md` | Bromberg, Lobatcheva & Peters (2017). Episodic future thinking reduces temporal discounting in healthy adolescents. *PLOS ONE*. |
| `hollis-hansen-2019.md` | Hollis-Hansen, O'Donnell, Seidman, Brande & Epstein (2019). Improvements in episodic future thinking methodology: Establishing a standardized episodic thinking control. *PLOS ONE*. |
| `calluso-2019.md` | Calluso, Tosoni, Cannito & Committeri (2019). Concreteness and emotional valence of episodic future thinking (EFT) independently affect the dynamics of intertemporal decisions. *PLOS ONE*. |
| `noreen-2022.md` | Noreen & Dritschel (2022). In the here and now: Future thinking and social problem-solving in depression. *PLOS ONE*. |
| `noreen-2023.md` | Noreen & Dritschel (2023). Thinking about the consequences: The detrimental role of future thinking on intrapersonal problem-solving in depression. *PLOS ONE*. |

**License:** all five are published in *PLOS ONE* under CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Abstracts are reproduced unchanged; credit goes to the authors listed in each file.

**The rule for the lab agent:** it may cite **only** papers in this folder. If it wants to mention any other paper, it says so and asks you to find and add it.

*For course maintainers:* this pack deliberately leaves out the paper that goes with the dataset in `data/` and its follow-ups, so the comparison in 3B.3b stays blind. Don't add them.
