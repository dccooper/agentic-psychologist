# Orientation video: voiceover script

*Recorded 2026-10-28. The finished video runs about 6½ minutes.*

**Title card:** University of Tomorrow · Department of Experimental Theoretical Psychology · *New Student Orientation (Video)* · "Replaces the orientation seminar."

**Voice:** you play **M. Pending, Acting Deputy Assistant Registrar (provisionally)**, the memo-writer from the home page. Dry, warm, unhurried; let the understatement do the work. Once, in the middle, you step out of character as yourself for the serious part.

**Humor rule (from the site's design guidelines):** jokes are on the institution (the coffee machine, the registrar, the paperwork, Procrastination Hall), never on the learner or clients. Safety, privacy, the trust prompt, and cost are **played straight**. Those lines are marked **[STRAIGHT]**.

## How to record

- One continuous recording is fine (a phone voice memo works). Find a quiet room with soft things around it (a closet of coats is ideal).
- **Read each numbered line, then pause about 2 seconds.** If you flub a line, pause and read it again; the pipeline keeps the last take.
- Don't read the line numbers or the *[on screen]* notes aloud.
- Several files are fine too (this video used four). Save them as WAV; the pipeline cuts the picture to your pacing. See `README.md`.

## The script

### Cold open: title card

**1.** Welcome to the University of Tomorrow, Department of Experimental Theoretical Psychology. This is your orientation video. It replaces the orientation seminar, which was cancelled when nobody could find the room.

**2.** I'm M. Pending, Acting Deputy Assistant Registrar. Provisionally. In the next five minutes, we'll get you set up with your tutor. You don't need any technical background. The department certainly doesn't have one.

### 1. Download the right app

*[on screen: the download page, mocked; the wrong section crossed out, the right one highlighted]*

**3.** Step one: download the right app. Google makes several things called Antigravity, because naming things is hard. Even for Google.

**4.** Go to antigravity dot google, slash download. The first thing on the page is called "Antigravity App." It is not the one. Scroll past it. Keep scrolling. Near the very bottom, in the smallest heading on the page, is "Antigravity IDE, Standalone." That's the one. We've filed a complaint about the font size.

**5.** Choose your kind of computer. On Windows, the x64 button suits almost everyone. On a Mac, choose Apple Silicon if your Mac has an M-series chip, and Intel if it's older. The written guide shows you how to check.

### 2. Install it

*[on screen: split screen, Windows | Mac]*

**6.** On Windows, open the installer and click Next until it stops asking. One exception: tick "Create a desktop icon," so you can find it again.

**7.** On a Mac, open the file and drag Antigravity IDE into your Applications folder. The first time you open it, your Mac may ask whether you're sure, since it came from the internet. You are. Click Open.

**8.** And that's the installation. The department's own building project, Procrastination Hall, has been installing for eleven years. Opening next semester, they say.

### 3. Sign in and the setup screens

*[on screen: "Continue with Google"; then the setup screens flipping past, with the agent-mode choice highlighted]*

**9.** Antigravity will ask you to sign in with Google. A personal account is simplest. University accounts sometimes block new apps, as university accounts do.

**10.** Then come a few setup screens. Choose "Start fresh." Pick any theme you like. And when it asks how you want to use the agent, choose "Review-driven development." That means the AI asks before it changes anything. Which is more than this office does.

### 4. Out of character: the serious part [STRAIGHT]

*[on screen: plain card, no crest. "The one rule" text appears with line 12.]*

**11.** A quick step out of character. I'm David Cooper. I'm a psychologist, and I made this course. A few things deserve a straight answer.

**12.** During setup, you'll see a security notice. In plain terms, it says that an AI agent that can read and change files can make mistakes, or be tricked into sending information somewhere it shouldn't. That's true of every tool like this. It's also why this course has one rule: never put real patient or research-participant data into this course, or into any AI tool your institution hasn't approved. Everything in the course is fictional or public.

**13.** The same screen asks whether Google can use your interactions to improve its products. That box is checked by default. It's your choice. Uncheck it if you prefer.

**14.** And the course is free. Antigravity's free plan has usage limits, so if you hit one, the lessons pause, and you pick up where you left off.

### Back in character: pickup

*[on screen: M. Pending's name card returns; a progress tracker with steps 1–3 done and step 4 next]*

**14a.** Ahem, okay, picking up where we left off.

### 5. Get the course and open it

*[on screen: the ZIP; Windows "Extract All" | Mac double-click; then Open Folder]*

**15.** Back in character. Next, download the course itself. The link is on the setup page. On Windows, right-click the ZIP file and choose "Extract All." Don't just open it; the files won't work from inside the ZIP. On a Mac, double-click it.

**16.** In Antigravity, choose "Open Folder," and pick the folder called "agentic-psychologist-main."

**17.** [STRAIGHT] It will ask whether you trust the authors of these files. Choose "Yes." The tutor needs that permission to do its job.

### 6. Your first look *(the prototype scene)*

*[on screen: the mock window; the three highlights; the "ignore for now" stripes]*

**18.** And here it is. It looks like the cockpit of a small aircraft. Don't panic. You only need three parts of it.

**19.** On the right, the agent panel. That's where you talk to your tutor.

**20.** On the left, the Explorer. It's the course's filing cabinet. Unlike the department's, everything in it is where it says it is.

**21.** In the middle, the editor. A file opens here when you click on it.

**22.** Everything else, you can safely ignore. We do.

### 7. Start the course

*[on screen: zoom to the message box; /sta; the Tab key; Enter]*

**23.** To begin, click in the message box and type a forward slash, then the letters s, t, a.

**24.** Press Tab to fill in the rest. This matters: on some computers, typing the whole thing doesn't work. Then press Enter.

### 8. Waiting

*[on screen: "Working…" spinner]*

**25.** Now, wait. Replies can take a minute or two on the free plan. That's normal, and you'll see it working. Please don't refresh. Refreshing is how the coffee machine broke.

### 9. Accept or reject

*[on screen: a mock change card: the highlighted change, with Accept and Reject buttons]*

**26.** When your tutor wants to change a file, it shows you exactly what it plans to change, and asks. "Accept" lets it make that one change. "Reject" means no, and nothing happens. Saying no is always safe.

**27.** It's like reviewing a trainee's note before you sign it. Read first. Then decide.

### Close: end card

*[on screen: crest; "From here, the tutor takes over."; the site address; /im-stuck and /my-progress]*

**28.** That's orientation. From here, your tutor takes you through it one step at a time, about twenty minutes a lesson. That's roughly how long the kettle takes, now that the coffee machine is temporarily out of order.

**29.** If you get stuck, type slash im-stuck. To see where you are, slash my-progress.

**30.** Welcome to the department. We're glad you're here. Provisionally.

## Notes for anyone re-recording

- **The recording is the source of truth for captions.** The narrator ad-libbed in places; captions show what was said (see `transcripts/en/`), cleaned up by the `captionFixes` list in `strings.en.js`.
- **Lines added later get a letter** (like **14a.**) so nothing is renumbered: line numbers are how scenes, anchors and overrides find their words.
- **Lines 11–14 are played straight,** out of character, with no music. Keep that tone in any translation.
- **Line 12 paraphrases Antigravity's security notice.** If the notice's wording changes, re-check the paraphrase.
