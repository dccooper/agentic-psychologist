# Lesson 4.2: Go Live: Make It Yours, Safely

- **Module:** 4 (capstone, shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~6 agent requests in the course window (the agent's window uses more; estimate)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested as a script (QA pass 5's improvised run skipped the rewrite: the agent's `AGENTS.md` kept its placeholders)

## Goal

The learner completes the **go-live checklist**, then rewrites their agent's instructions for their **real life** from a brain dump (typed or spoken), runs one skill on real, non-patient material, and refines a skill by telling the agent what to change.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

This is the moment the course's rules become your rules. What data does it touch, where does it go, what does your institution allow, and what must it never do? Answering that before the first real use is the same professional judgment as checking consent before a session.

## Setup

- `lessons/4-capstone/go-live-checklist.md` (the template). The learner copies it to `workspace/capstone/go-live.md`.
- The agent's folder from 4.1. **The professor doesn't see it.** The learner brings back evidence.
- **Coach mode starts here.** Ask, don't prescribe. **Never write the brain dump or the prompt for them.** If they're stuck, ask questions ("Who are you at work? What do you juggle? What drives you mad?"), not sentences to paste.
- **Model for a good result** (✅ from QA pass 5's agent file): sections for role and working style, scope, a **Commands** table, a **Red Line** (data privacy), **action boundaries** (draft and suggest only; never send, book, or delete), and "flag anything unclear." **No `[placeholder]` brackets left.**

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: switching windows ⚠️; the microphone (voice input) ⚠️; reviewing changes; **+** for a new conversation.

## Steps

1. **The checklist (course window).** Have them copy the template to `workspace/capstone/go-live.md` and fill in sections 1–6 **in their own words**. Go through it with them one section at a time, briefly. Confirm each answer is real (not "fine"). For section 2, help them find Antigravity's data-use setting ⚠️. For section 4, the stop sign: if their real use would touch protected data, this agent isn't the tool for that, full stop.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `go-live.md`. All six sections are answered, the stop-sign box is ticked, and the red line is specific (not "be careful").
   - **Knowledge check:** *"Your real calendar export has client names on it. Can you attach it if you delete the names first?"* (Expected: **no.** Times, places, and appointment patterns can still identify people, and export files carry hidden details. Type the week in by hand, with client time as "clinical block." This is 0.2 again.)

2. **Rewrite the instructions from a brain dump (agent's window).** Save `progress.md` at "4.2, step 3," then: *"In your agent's window, tell it about you, out loud or typed, messy is fine: who you are, what you juggle, how you like things, and what it must never do. Ask it to rewrite `AGENTS.md` from that, with your red line pasted in word for word. Then ask it: what am I missing? When you're done, come back here and type `/next-step`."* **Pause point.**

3. **Back: show me.** Ask them to paste (or read out) their agent's new `AGENTS.md` headings and red line.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the red line matches `go-live.md` word for word; **no `[placeholder]` brackets remain**; it says what the agent must never do. If placeholders remain, the rewrite didn't happen. Send them back, kindly.

4. **First real run, then fix by telling (agent's window).** One skill on real, non-patient material: their actual week (typed in by hand, client time as "clinical block") or two papers they're reading. Something won't be quite right. They tell the agent what to change in the skill in plain words ("shorter, and personal stuff last"), review the change, and rerun. Then they run their wrap-up skill. Before they go: *"Come back with `/next-step` when you've rerun it."*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they paste one line of the real output (or describe it) and say what they changed in the skill. If there's no evidence, it didn't happen yet: say so warmly and let them finish.

5. **Debrief (coach questions).** *"What surprised you? What wouldn't you trust it with yet?"* Listen; one observation back, at most.

6. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You didn't write a careful prompt. You talked, it organized, and you set the rules it lives by. You're the one who knows what good looks like."*

## Pause points

- After step 2 (switching to the agent's window).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/capstone/go-live.md` is complete; the agent's `AGENTS.md` is rewritten from their brain dump, with the red line word for word and no placeholders; one real run happened and one skill was changed by telling, **with evidence brought back**.

## Common sticking points

- **"I don't know my institution's AI policy"** → that's the answer to write down, plus "ask [who] before real use." Then use only clearly non-sensitive material (their own to-dos, published papers).
- **They want the professor to write the brain dump** → no. Ask three questions instead. Messy is the point: the agent organizes.
- **The agent asks for a calendar export or a client list** → the red line in its `AGENTS.md` should stop it. If it doesn't, they tighten the red line by telling it.
- **They're testing, with no real life to put in** → fine to invent a plausible role, but still do every step, and still no patient data.

## Avoid

- Never write the learner's brain dump, prompt, red line, or debrief answers.
- Never mark a step done without evidence from the agent's window.
- No patient or client data, ever, including "just to test."
