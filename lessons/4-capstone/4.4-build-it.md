# Lesson 4.4: Build It

- **Module:** 4 (capstone, shared)
- **Time:** open-ended, in ~20-minute chunks
- **Request budget:** ~2 agent requests per check-in in the course window; the build happens in the agent's window
- **Last tested:** not yet tested as a script (QA pass 5's improvised run declared the course complete **without anything being built**; this script requires evidence)

## Goal

The learner builds the first version of their project **in their agent's window**, in short chunks, each ending with a handoff note, and uses it once for real. The professor is a coach they can check in with, not a guide.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Real projects get built in the gaps between sessions, supervision, and teaching. Twenty minutes, a handoff note, stop; come back next week and pick up exactly where you were. That's the habit that makes agents useful in a real professional life.

## Setup

- The learner's brief and examples of good from 4.3, in the agent's folder (or the project's own folder).
- `workspace/capstone/build-log.md`: one line per chunk (date, what got done). The learner writes it, or pastes the line from their handoff note.
- **Coach mode.** At a check-in, ask three questions and give **one** observation. **Don't hand out the next step or write prompts.**
- **The finish line needs evidence.** The professor can't see the agent's folder. The lesson is done only when the learner shows the first version working: a pasted output, a file list, or a description specific enough to check (what they ran, what it produced).

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: switching windows ⚠️; the microphone ⚠️; pasting screenshots ⚠️.

## Steps

1. **How a chunk works.** Two or three sentences: *"Each chunk: your agent reads its handoff, you build one thing, you look at it, you tell it what to change, then you run your wrap-up. Twenty minutes, then stop. Come back here any time with `/next-step` for a check-in."* Remind them of new-skill timing: a skill they create shows up in a **new** conversation.
   - **When they're stuck, in this order:** ask the agent ("explain what you just did," "what am I missing?"); diagnose by part (forgets → memory, inconsistent → skill, ignores preferences → instructions); then `/im-stuck` here, bringing the handoff note.
   - Save `progress.md` at "4.4, building: chunk 1," then they go.

2. **Check-ins (each time they come back with `/next-step`).** Ask:
   1. *"What did you build?"*
   2. *"What surprised you?"*
   3. *"What will you check before you trust it?"*

   Give one observation, at most. Have them add a line to `build-log.md`, and update `progress.md` with the chunk count and their own one-line status. Then: *"Ready for another chunk?"*
   - **Knowledge check** (at the first check-in): *"You come back after two weeks, and your agent has no idea what you did last time. Which part failed, and where do you look first?"* (Expected: **memory.** Check `memory/handoff.md` first: was the wrap-up skill run? If it was, check the **skill**: does it read the handoff?)

3. **The finish line.** When they say the first version works, ask them to **show it**: paste an output, list the project's files, or describe exactly what they ran and what came out. And: *"Have you used it once, for real?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** evidence of a working first version, **one real use**, a current `memory/handoff.md` (they can paste its first lines), and at least one line in `build-log.md`. Without evidence, it isn't done yet: say so warmly and offer another chunk.

4. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You built something real, in short chunks, and your agent knew where you'd left off every time. You built that habit into it yourself."*

## Pause points

- Every chunk ends at a natural stop (the wrap-up). That's the design of this lesson.

## Done when

The learner has shown a working first version, used it once for real, and has a current handoff note and a build log.

## Common sticking points

- **They come back with nothing built** → no judgment. Ask what got in the way, and shrink the next chunk ("What's ten minutes of progress?").
- **The agent built something different from the brief** → compare its work with the brief (the 2.3 debugging move), then fix by telling.
- **They want to keep going past the first version** → wonderful. The lesson ends at the first version used for real; the project doesn't have to.

## Avoid

- Don't write prompts, briefs, or next steps for them. Coach.
- **Never mark this lesson, or the course, complete without evidence of a working first version.**
- Don't read the agent's folder; ask for what you need.
