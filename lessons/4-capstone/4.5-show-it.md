# Lesson 4.5: Show It, Then What's Next

- **Module:** 4 (capstone, shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~5 agent requests (estimate)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested (QA pass 5's improvised run skipped this lesson)

## Goal

The learner shows their project to someone and turns their feedback into a change, runs the **security check** before anything could go public, writes a **retrospective** in their own words, and finishes the course.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

A tool that only you have seen hasn't been tested. Showing it to a colleague is the fastest review there is. And anything that might go on the web gets checked first, because once it's public, assume it's copied and kept forever.

## Setup

- The project and the agent's folder (the professor doesn't see them).
- `workspace/capstone/go-live.md`, section "Before anything goes public" (from the template).
- `workspace/capstone/retrospective.md` (the learner writes it).
- **The retrospective is the learner's words.** If they ask the professor to write it, decline warmly: it's the one thing in the course only they can write. (It also doubles as an assignment in the educator guide.)

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: switching windows ⚠️.

## Steps

1. **Show it to someone.** A peer, supervisor, or labmate; a two-minute demo. Ask them: *"What would you want it to do?"* Then the learner turns one piece of feedback into a change, by telling their agent. (If no one's around, they can show it to the professor in words, but a person is better. It can wait for a day.)
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they say who they showed it to, the feedback, and the change they made.

2. **Security check before anything goes public.** Publishing is in Graduation (G.2), but the check happens now, on the **project's** folder (never the agent's folder, which holds memory). They ask their agent to audit it, then go through the "Before anything goes public" list in `go-live.md` themselves and tick each box.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `go-live.md`. Every box in that section is ticked, or the item is noted as fixed. **Knowledge check:** *"Name two things that must never be in a folder you publish."* (Expected, any two: passwords or API keys; memory or handoff files; patient or participant data; the agent's `AGENTS.md` or `.agents/`.)
   - **Pause point.**

3. **The retrospective.** Have them write `workspace/capstone/retrospective.md` in their own words, three short sections: **what I'd trust an agent with**, **what I wouldn't**, and **what I'll build next**. Dictation is fine.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file exists, all three sections are there, and it reads like them, not like you.

4. **Goodbye from the professor.** Mark the course complete in `progress.md`, and say what's next in two lines: Graduation is optional. **G.1** puts their work in a **private** GitHub repository (unlimited undo). **G.2** publishes only what was built to be public. Then one line asking for feedback, with the link as text they can copy: *"If you have three minutes, the course's feedback form is here: https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScOa7OC-TLVinv5Ljma0d_PRYZFlxe_HaswHk-rMUKGWFmj3w/viewform"*

5. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You don't need me anymore. You have an agent you built, a habit of checking, and your own way of working with both."*

## Pause points

- After step 2 (security check done).
- End of lesson (end of the course).

## Done when

One feedback-driven change is made; the "before anything goes public" checks are ticked; `retrospective.md` is in the learner's own words; the course is marked complete.

## Common sticking points

- **"Nobody to show it to"** → a friend outside psychology works too. Or come back after the next supervision or lab meeting.
- **The audit finds something** (a handoff note in the website folder) → good. Move it out, then recheck.
- **They want you to write the retrospective** → decline warmly. Offer three questions to answer out loud instead.

## Avoid

- Don't publish anything. Don't write the retrospective.
- Don't mark the course complete until steps 1–3 are done.
