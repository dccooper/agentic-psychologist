# Lesson 1.4: First Real Tasks

- **Module:** 1 (shared)
- **Time:** ~25 min
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner produces two pieces of real work, an action-items file and a reply email, and **revises each one instead of accepting the first draft**. They finish each piece with their own hand edits.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Most of a clinician's or researcher's non-clinical day is exactly this: turning meetings into tasks, and writing emails to busy people. AI drafts fast, and the first draft is rarely the one to send. Revising (and knowing what to ask for) is the skill.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/dept-meeting-transcript.md` (from 1.3; expected action items are listed in `lessons/1-foundations/1.3-context-with-at.md` → Setup)
- `world/artifacts/okafor-shared-drive-email.md` (Dr. Okafor's "Thoughts?" email from 1.2)
- `workspace/drive-plan.md` (the learner's plan from 1.2). **If it's missing**, the reply can be based on the email alone. Don't recreate the plan.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: **@** to attach files; reviewing and accepting a new file; editing and saving by hand.

## Steps

**Part 1: Action items**

1. **Pick your format.** Ask: *"When you get a list of action items, what do you want it to look like?"* Help them decide in a minute or two: columns (who, what, when?), how to show missing owners, whether decisions get their own section. *Why:* this becomes **their** format, and in 2.1 they'll save it so they never have to explain it again.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've described a format in their own words.

2. **First draft.** Have them ask the AI, using the four parts from 1.3, to turn `@dept-meeting-transcript.md` into action items **in their format**, saved to `workspace/meeting-actions.md`. Write it; they review and accept it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file exists. Read it and compare with the expected answers in 1.3: five owned items, the coffee machine **unassigned**, the AI-guidance item flagged as unclear, and the 60-minute meetings listed as a **decision**, not an action.

3. **Revise once.** Ask: *"What would make this more useful?"* If they're unsure, offer two options: make each task start with a verb, or turn relative dates into real ones ("tomorrow" → the 4th; the meeting was Monday the 3rd). Have them ask the AI for that change, then review it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file has changed in the way they asked for.

4. **Finish it by hand.** Have them make at least one edit themselves (a word, an order, a note). It's their file now. **Pause point.**
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the file and confirm a learner edit is there.

**Part 2: The reply**

5. **First draft.** Have them ask the AI to draft a reply to Dr. Okafor's `@okafor-shared-drive-email.md`, proposing the plan in `@drive-plan.md`, saved to `workspace/drafts/reply-to-okafor.md`. Write it; they review and accept it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the draft exists. Ask them to read it as Dr. Okafor would. *"Would a busy chair read all of this?"*

6. **Revise: shorter, warmer, more direct.** Have them ask for **one** revision, choosing the direction themselves: shorter, warmer, more direct, or a mix. Then a second revision if they want. *Why:* telling the AI *how* to change a draft is faster than rewriting it, and you stay the author.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they asked for at least one revision and can say what changed.

7. **Make it sound like you.** Have them edit the final draft by hand until they'd be happy to sign it. Check one thing together: **does the reply promise anything the plan doesn't say?** (For example, "I've already moved the files." Dr. Okafor said not to.)
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the draft; there's a learner edit, and it doesn't claim files were moved.

8. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"Neither first draft was the one you kept. You told the AI how to improve it, then finished it yourself, so the work is yours and it's better than either of you would've made alone."*

## Pause points

- After step 4 (action items finished).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/meeting-actions.md` and `workspace/drafts/reply-to-okafor.md` both exist, each has been revised at least once through the AI and edited at least once by hand, and the reply doesn't claim anything was moved.

## Common sticking points

- **They accept the first draft as "fine"** → ask them to read it aloud in their head as the recipient. Fine isn't the bar. Would they sign it?
- **The revision overshoots** (too short, too casual) → great. Ask for a nudge back ("a bit warmer than that"). Steering in small steps is the skill.
- **The AI adds things that aren't in the plan** → have them reject that part or ask for it to be removed. Check against the source, as in 1.3.
- **They want to send the email** → this is practice in a fictional world. Nothing gets sent. For real email, the habit is the same: draft here, paste into your email, read once more before sending.

## Avoid

- Don't write the revisions they're supposed to ask for, and don't do the hand edits.
- Don't send or pretend to send anything.
- Don't save a template or rule yet. That's 2.1.
