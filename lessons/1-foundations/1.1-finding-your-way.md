# Lesson 1.1: Finding Your Way Around the Editor

- **Module:** 1 (shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity IDE 2.5.5, 2026-10-24, QA pass 2 (David). Passed. Review step rewritten from the observed UI; needs a retest.

## Goal

The learner can create a folder and a file themselves, have the AI edit a file, and **review the AI's changes before keeping them**.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

An AI that can change your files is powerful, and you stay in charge by reviewing what it changed. Same idea as reviewing a trainee's note before signing it.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/jordan-survival-guide.md`
- Interface vocabulary to use consistently: **Explorer** (the list of folders and files), **editor** (where open files show, as tabs), **agent panel** (where you and the learner talk). Lead with these labels.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: the window's areas; New Folder / New File in the Explorer; Markdown as editable text + **Open Preview**; saving; **reviewing an AI edit** (✅ verified in QA pass 2: the Review card, per-change Accept/Reject, the Accept Changes bar, Files With Changes / Accept all). If the learner's screen differs, ask what they see, then note it under "Notes" in `progress.md` so the screen guide can be fixed.

## Steps

1. **The three areas.** Name them by what they're for, one line each: the **agent panel** (where we're talking), the **Explorer** (the course's filing cabinet), and the **editor** (where a file opens when you click it). Mention that panels can be moved or hidden, so names matter more than places. Give them permission to ignore the rest, in one line: *"You'll see plenty of other icons and bars. You can ignore all of them. I'll mention anything you need."* Then connect it to the term from setup in one sentence: *"Files, editor, and AI in one window. That's what makes this an **IDE**, an integrated development environment."* Ask them to find each one and tell you what's around it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've found all three. If one is missing, help with `SCREEN_GUIDE.md`.

2. **Make a folder.** Have them create a folder named `my-notes` *inside* `workspace`: in the Explorer, right-click `workspace` and choose **New Folder…**, type `my-notes`, and press Enter. If that option isn't there, ask what they see. *Why:* you organize your work, and the AI works inside that organization.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/my-notes/` exists.

3. **Make a file and type in it.** Have them right-click `my-notes`, choose **New File…**, name it `first-week.md`, and type:
   ```
   # My First Week
   ```
   followed by one line about how they're feeling about starting. Save it (Ctrl+S / Cmd+S). A dot on the tab means it isn't saved yet.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file exists and has their text.

   **Then, a just-in-time explanation of Markdown** (keep it short, after they've typed it):
   > *"The `.md` ending means **Markdown**. It's ordinary text with a few shortcuts for formatting: `#` makes a heading, `-` starts a list item, `**double asterisks**` make bold. AI tools use Markdown for notes, plans, instructions, and documentation because both people and AI can read and edit it easily. Even this course's lessons are Markdown files. That's all you need for now. If you're curious, here's a one-page guide: https://www.markdownguide.org/basic-syntax/"*

   Optionally: right-click the file's tab and choose **Open Preview** (or Ctrl+Shift+V) to see it formatted.

4. **Read Jordan's guide.** Have them open `world/artifacts/jordan-survival-guide.md` and skim it. It's messy on purpose.

5. **Ask the AI to edit their file.** Have them ask, in their own words, for the AI to turn Jordan's guide into a **clean, prioritized week-1 checklist** and **add it to `first-week.md`** below their own text. Let them try writing the request first. If it's vague, that's fine. Do what they asked, and you can talk about better prompts in 1.3.

   **Don't update `progress.md` in the same turn as this edit.** Keep the change to just their file, so the review is simple. Update progress after they've reviewed.

6. **Review the change.** This is the key moment of the lesson. Antigravity shows the change in three places (see "Review the AI's changes" in `SCREEN_GUIDE.md`). Walk them through it **one step at a time**:
   1. *"Under my message there's a small card: **1 file changed +20 −1** with a **Review** button. Click **Review**. It opens `first-week.md` with my changes highlighted."* (Green-tinted lines are additions. Red-tinted lines are being removed.)
   2. *"Read the highlighted part. Is your heading and your line about how you feel still there? Is anything from Jordan's guide missing, or did I invent something?"* Ask them to read the text, not just glance at colors.
   3. *"Each highlighted block has its own small **Accept** / **Reject** buttons, so you can keep some parts and not others. The bar at the bottom of the editor (**Accept Changes** / **Reject**) does the whole file at once."* Have them choose deliberately.
   4. If they reject it, help them ask again more precisely ("add it below my text, don't change my text").

   Then explain the bar above the message box in two sentences: *"**N Files With Changes** lists every file waiting for your OK, here maybe `progress.md` too, which is me saving your place. **Accept all** keeps everything listed. It's handy once you've looked, but it's a shortcut for things you've already checked, not a substitute for checking."*

   If the change was applied without asking (for example, they chose **Agent-driven development** at setup), use it as the lesson anyway: open the file, check it the same way, and use **Ctrl+Z** in the file to undo if needed. Mention that the review setting decides whether the AI asks first (Lesson 1.2 covers it).
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the checklist is in `first-week.md`, and they confirmed they reviewed it and chose to keep it.

7. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You just directed an AI to edit a real file, and you checked its work before keeping it. That 'review before you accept' habit is the most important one in this whole course."* Then update `progress.md`.

## Pause points

- After step 3 (folder and file created): a safe break.
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/my-notes/first-week.md` exists with their own text plus an AI-made checklist they reviewed and kept.

## Common sticking points

- **The file was created in the wrong place** → reassure them. They can drag it into the right folder in the Explorer.
- **No "New Folder…" / "New File…" option** → ask what the right-click menu shows. Try hovering over the Explorer heading for small new-file/new-folder icons. Note what happened for the course authors.
- **They can't type in the file** → they may be in the Preview tab. Close it and click the file in the Explorer.
- **They can't find accept/reject** → point them to the **Review** button on the card under your message, or the **Files With Changes** bar above the message box.
- **Pending changes piling up** (several files in the Files With Changes bar) → explain it's a to-do list of edits waiting for their OK. Review their own file first. `progress.md` is safe to accept after a glance.
- **The AI overwrote their text** → perfect teaching moment. Reject or undo it, and ask again with "add below my text, don't change it."
- **"Did I break it?"** → almost nothing in this course can't be undone. Undo (Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z) works in files too.

## Avoid

- Don't create the folder or file for them. They do steps 2–3 by hand.
- Don't introduce review settings or @-mentions in depth yet (1.2 and 1.3).
- Don't silently "fix" their prompt. Do roughly what they asked.
- Don't turn the Markdown note into a tutorial. Three sentences and a link.
