# Lesson 1.1: Finding Your Way Around Cursor

- **Module:** 1 (shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner can create a folder and a file themselves, have the AI edit a file, and **review and accept (or reject) the AI's changes**.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

An AI that can change your files is powerful, and you stay in charge by reviewing what it changed. Same idea as reviewing a trainee's note before signing it.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/jordan-survival-guide.md`
- Interface vocabulary to use consistently: **file panel** (left), **editor** (middle, where files open), **chat** (where they talk to you).

**Upkeep rule:** describe interface elements by what they look like and where they are, not exact menu paths. Cursor's UI changes. If what the learner sees doesn't match your description, ask them what they *do* see and adapt.

## Steps

1. **The three areas.** Briefly name the file panel, editor, and chat, one line each. Ask them to point out (describe) each one.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've described all three.

2. **Make a folder.** Have them create a folder named `my-notes` *inside* `workspace`, using the file panel (right-click on `workspace`, or the new-folder icon at the top of the panel). *Why:* you organize your work, and the AI works inside that organization.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/my-notes/` exists.

3. **Make a file and type in it.** Have them create `first-week.md` inside `my-notes`, and type a heading like `# My First Week` and one line about how they're feeling about starting. Save it. *Why:* files are just text. Nothing magic. (One line on `.md`: it's "Markdown," plain text with a few symbols for formatting, and it's what AI tools read and write best.)
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file exists and has their text.

4. **Read Jordan's guide.** Have them open `world/artifacts/jordan-survival-guide.md` and skim it. It's messy on purpose.

5. **Ask the AI to edit their file.** Have them ask, in their own words, for the AI to turn Jordan's guide into a **clean, prioritized week-1 checklist** and **add it to `first-week.md`** below their own text. Let them try writing the request first. If it's vague, that's fine. Do what they asked, and you can talk about better prompts in 1.3.

   When you make the change, **it will be shown as a proposed change** they can review.

6. **Review the change.** This is the key moment of the lesson. Explain: the AI's changes show up as additions and deletions (often green and red) that they can **accept or reject**. Ask them to look at it before accepting:
   - Did it keep their own text?
   - Is anything from Jordan's guide missing or invented?

   Then they accept (or reject and ask again).
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the checklist is in `first-week.md`, and they confirmed they reviewed it.

7. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You just directed an AI to edit a real file, and you checked its work before keeping it. That 'review before you accept' habit is the most important one in this whole course."*

## Pause points

- After step 3 (folder and file created): a safe break.
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/my-notes/first-week.md` exists with their own text plus an AI-made checklist they reviewed and accepted.

## Common sticking points

- **The file was created in the wrong place** → reassure them. Show them they can drag it into the right folder in the file panel.
- **They can't find the accept/reject controls** → ask them to describe what they see near the change or at the top of the chat/editor. Adapt to the current UI.
- **The AI overwrote their text** → perfect teaching moment. Show them how to reject or undo, and ask again with "add below my text, don't change it."
- **"Did I break it?"** → almost nothing in this course can't be undone. Mention undo (Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z) works in files too.

## Avoid

- Don't create the folder or file for them. They do steps 2–3 by hand.
- Don't introduce modes or @-mentions yet (1.2 and 1.3), even if they could help.
- Don't silently "fix" their prompt. Do roughly what they asked.
