# Lesson 2.1: Templates and Rules

- **Module:** 2 (shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~6 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner saves their action-items format as a **template**, writes a **rule** the AI follows in every conversation, and sees the AI use both **without being reminded**.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Every practice and lab has "the way we do it here": note formats, email tone, what never gets guessed. Writing that down once, where the AI reads it every time, saves the explaining and makes the output consistent. It's the AI version of a style guide or a clinic policy binder (the one you can actually find).

## Setup

- `workspace/meeting-actions.md` (the learner's finished action items from 1.4). **If it's missing**, have them describe their preferred format instead and build the template from that.
- `workspace/my-rule.md` (the data rule they wrote for *themselves* in 0.2, used as a callback)
- `world/artifacts/jordan-survival-guide.md` (the test material in step 5; its "things to do week 1" list has no owners and only one date, **"by friday"** for the supervision sign-up)
- **Rule file format (Antigravity):** rules live in `.agents/rules/` as `.md` files and **must start with this frontmatter**, or Antigravity silently ignores them:
  ```
  ---
  trigger: always_on
  ---
  ```
  ⚠️ *Verify in testing that `always_on` rules are picked up, and whether that needs a new conversation.*

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: New Folder / New File in the Explorer (inside the `.agents` folder); editing and saving by hand; reviewing and accepting a new file; **+** for a new conversation; **@** to attach a file.

## Steps

1. **The repeat problem.** One or two sentences: *"In 1.4 you explained your format to the AI. Next week you'd have to explain it again, and again. Today you write it down once."*

2. **Make a template.** Have them ask the AI to turn `@meeting-actions.md` into a **blank template** at `workspace/templates/action-items.md`: the same layout and headings, with placeholders instead of real tasks. They review and accept it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `workspace/templates/action-items.md`. It has the learner's layout and **no real action items** from the meeting.

3. **A rule for yourself, a rule for the AI.** Callback: *"In 0.2 you wrote a rule for yourself in `my-rule.md`. A rule for the AI is the same idea, written where the AI reads it every time."*

4. **Write the rule by hand.** Have them create the folder `rules` inside `.agents` (right-click `.agents` → **New Folder…**), then the file `my-style.md` inside it. **They type it**, starting with the frontmatter exactly as shown in Setup, then two or three rules in their own words. They should cover:
   - how they like writing to sound (e.g., "plain language, short sentences");
   - "action items always have an owner and a due date; if either is missing, write *unassigned* or *no date*, never guess";
   - "when I ask for action items, use the format in `workspace/templates/action-items.md`."
   - *Optional:* add the gist of their 0.2 rule ("if I'm about to share client information, stop me and remind me why"), so the AI backs them up.

   Save. *Why the frontmatter:* it tells Antigravity when to use the rule. `always_on` means every conversation. Without it, the file is ignored, silently.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `.agents/rules/my-style.md`. It starts with the three frontmatter lines, has the owner/date rule, and points to the template. Fix typos in the frontmatter together (it must be exact). **Pause point.**

5. **Test it without mentioning it.** Have them start a **new conversation** (the **+**) and ask: *"Turn the 'things to do week 1' list in @jordan-survival-guide.md into action items."* **Nothing about the format, the template, or the rule.** Then come back here by typing `/start-course` in that new conversation.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** ask them what they got, or read the file if it saved one. Expected: **their template's layout**, the supervision sign-up **due Friday**, and everything else marked **no date** (not invented dates). The owner is the new trainee (them), or unassigned.

6. **Name the part.** One line, as a preview of the next lesson: *"A rule is part of an AI's **instructions**: what it should do every time, without being asked. Next lesson, you'll open the instructions that have been running this course."*

7. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"The AI followed your format and your rules without being reminded, because you wrote them down where it looks. Any preference you find yourself repeating is a candidate for a template or a rule."*

## Pause points

- After step 4 (the rule is written).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/templates/action-items.md` exists; `.agents/rules/my-style.md` exists with correct frontmatter and the owner/date rule; and, in a fresh conversation, the AI used their format and didn't invent dates without being told to.

## Common sticking points

- **The rule doesn't seem to apply** → check, in order: the frontmatter is exactly three lines (`---`, `trigger: always_on`, `---`) at the very top; the file is saved (no dot on the tab); it's in `.agents/rules/`, not `.agents/skills/` or `workspace/`; and the test ran in a **new** conversation. If it still doesn't apply, note it in `progress.md` for the course authors.
- **They can't find `.agents`** → see the 2.2 sticking point: folders starting with a dot are sometimes hidden.
- **The AI invents dates anyway** → good teaching moment. Rules make good behavior more likely, not guaranteed. Reviewing still matters (1.1).
- **They write a rule that changes how the tutor teaches** (e.g., "always give me the answer") → explain gently that rules apply to every conversation in this folder, including the tutor. Keep rules about *their writing*, and leave the teaching rules to `AGENTS.md`.

## Avoid

- Don't write the rule file for them. Typing the frontmatter themselves is part of the lesson.
- Don't open `AGENTS.md` or explain skills yet. That's the reveal in 2.2.
- Don't mention the template or rule when they run the test in step 5.
