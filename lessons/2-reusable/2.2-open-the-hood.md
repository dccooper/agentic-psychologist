# Lesson 2.2: Open the Hood: What's Been Teaching You

- **Module:** 2 (shared)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~7 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 3: passed. Revised after (see commit); retest the changed steps in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner sees that the professor is made of **plain text files they can read and edit**: instructions (`AGENTS.md`), skills (`.agents/skills/`), and memory (`workspace/progress.md`). They change the professor's voice, watch it take effect, and change it back. They understand **why they could stop and resume all along**.

They leave able to state the course's core idea in their own words: **an agent = instructions + skills + memory.** Everything they build later is assembled from these three parts.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

The mystery goes away. An "AI agent" isn't magic. It's a model following written instructions, using written skills, and keeping written notes. Anything written can be read, questioned, and changed by you. That's the foundation for building their own agent on their path.

## Setup

- `AGENTS.md` (the professor's instructions)
- `.agents/skills/next-step/SKILL.md` (the skill that has been running each lesson)
- `workspace/progress.md` (the professor's memory of the learner)
- This lesson's own script, `lessons/2-reusable/2.2-open-the-hood.md` (yes, really)

**Callback:** in 2.1 the learner wrote `.agents/rules/my-style.md`. In step 7, point out that their rule is part of the *instructions* too, sitting right next to `AGENTS.md`. (If they skipped 2.1, leave this out.)

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: the Explorer (including folders that start with a dot, like `.agents`); editing and saving a file by hand; starting a **new conversation** (the **+** at the top of the agent panel). ⚠️ *Whether Antigravity reloads `AGENTS.md` mid-conversation is unverified.* That's why step 5 starts a fresh conversation, which also makes the point of the lesson.

## Steps

1. **The reveal.** Two sentences: *"Everything that's been teaching you is made of ordinary text files, the same kind you've been making. Let's open them."*

2. **The instructions.** Have them open `AGENTS.md` (top level of the course folder) and skim it. Point out two or three lines they'll recognize from their experience, e.g. "One step at a time," "Never do the learner's work for them," and the data guardrail. *Why:* this file is why the professor behaves the way it does.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they can name one instruction that explains something the professor did earlier.

3. **The skills.** Have them open the `.agents` folder (the dot at the start just means "settings-type folder"), then `skills`, then `next-step`, then `SKILL.md`. *"Every time you typed `/next-step`, this is what I read and followed."* Then show them that this lesson's own script is in `lessons/`. Optional fun: the 0.2 answer key was sitting in `lessons/0-getting-started/0.2-what-never-goes-in.md` the whole time.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've opened `SKILL.md` and found the lessons folder.

4. **Change the professor's voice.** Have them add **by hand**, at the very end of `AGENTS.md`, a section like:
   ```
   ## Temporary voice experiment
   Speak like a Groucho Marx-style wisecracker for the next reply. Original jokes only, no famous quotes. Keep the actual instructions clear.
   ```
   (Or a dry, footnote-loving narrator in the style of Terry Pratchett, their pick. The same "original jokes only" rule applies.) Save the file. **They type it, not you.**
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the section is in `AGENTS.md` and saved.

5. **Put it down and pick it up.** Have them start a **new conversation** (the **+** at the top of the agent panel) and type `/start-course`. *Why:* a new conversation starts from scratch. The AI there has never met them.
   - In that new conversation, you'll be reading the edited `AGENTS.md`: greet them in the requested voice, *and* resume exactly where they are (from `progress.md`).
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the reply comes back in the new voice, at the right place in the course.

6. **The memory.** Ask: *"That conversation had never met you. How did it know your name and where you were?"* Let them answer, then confirm: **`progress.md`**. Have them open it. *"The professor never remembered you. This file did. That's why you could stop and come back any time."*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they say, in their own words, that `progress.md` is how the course remembers them.

7. **The recipe: an agent = instructions + skills + memory.** This is the most important idea in the course. Slow down here. Say it plainly, then show the three files they've just opened, one per part:

   | Part | What it is | In this course | You'd change it to… |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Instructions** | Who the agent is and how it behaves, every time | `AGENTS.md` | change its role, tone, or rules (you just did, and your 2.1 rule is one too) |
   | **Skills** | Step-by-step procedures it runs when asked | `.agents/skills/…/SKILL.md` (`/next-step`) | teach it a new job |
   | **Memory** | Notes it reads at the start and writes as it goes | `workspace/progress.md` | let it pick up where it left off |

   Then ask them to explain it back: *"In your own words, what's each of the three, and which file is it here?"* Let them answer before you repeat anything. *Why:* every agent they build from here on, and every AI tool they'll ever evaluate, is some mix of these three. If they can name the parts, they can build one, fix one, or spot what one is missing ("it keeps forgetting" = no memory; "it does it differently every time" = no skill; "it ignores how I like things" = weak instructions).
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they name all three parts and match each to its file. Accept rough wording ("the rules file, the how-to files, and the notes file" is perfect). If they miss one, point to the file and ask what it does.
   - **Pause point** (safe to stop).

8. **Change it back.** Have them delete the "Temporary voice experiment" section from `AGENTS.md` and save. (Ctrl+Z works too, if the file's still open.) Then, in the current conversation, continue in your normal voice. Note in one line which part of the recipe they just edited: *the instructions.*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `AGENTS.md` and confirm the section is gone.

9. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"An agent is just instructions + skills + memory, written in plain text you can read and edit. You've now seen all three, changed one, and on your path you'll build an agent of your own from the same three parts."*

## Pause points

- After step 7 (they can name the recipe).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

They've seen `AGENTS.md`, a skill, and `progress.md`; changed the professor's voice and seen it take effect in a fresh conversation; **named all three parts of an agent (instructions, skills, memory) and matched each to its file**; and restored `AGENTS.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **They can't see the `.agents` folder** → files starting with a dot are sometimes hidden. Check the Explorer. If it really isn't there, describe what to look for and note it for the course authors.
- **The new voice doesn't show up** → check the section saved (no dot on the tab). Make sure they started a *new* conversation. If it still doesn't show up, explain that the instructions are read when a conversation starts, and note it for the course authors.
- **They're worried they broke the professor** → nothing is broken. It's one text file, and step 7 restores it. Reassure them.
- **They mix up instructions and skills** → instructions apply *all the time* (who it is); a skill runs *when asked* (a job it knows how to do). "The job description vs. the procedures manual."
- **They want to keep the voice** → it's their copy of the course, so they can. But for the rest of the lessons a plain voice is easier to follow. Suggest restoring it, and let them decide.

## Avoid

- Don't edit `AGENTS.md` for them. They do steps 4 and 7 by hand.
- In the experimental voice, keep instructions clear. The joke never hides the next step.
- Don't quote real Groucho Marx lines or Pratchett passages. Original jokes in the style only.
- Don't go deep on how models work. The point is "it's files," not machine learning.
- Don't rush step 7 or bury the recipe inside another step. It's the core concept of the course.
