# Lesson 3A.2a: Build a Chief of Staff: The Office

- **Module:** 3A (clinic)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 4: passed. Revised from the tester's notes; retest in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner builds a working **chief of staff agent** for Dr. Reyes from the three parts: **instructions** (a role file), **memory** (projects and people), and a first **skill** (`/weekly-plan`). They run it and get a weekly plan that fits the real calendar. They refine what the AI drafts mainly by **telling the AI what to change**, in their own words.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Clinic directors, private-practice clinicians, and supervisors all run on scattered lists. A chief of staff agent is one of the most useful AI projects a clinician can build, and it never needs client information: it organizes *your* work, not your clients' records.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/reyes-todo-pile.md` (four overlapping to-do lists, typed up by Priya; includes one client callback written safely: "details in chart, not here")
- `world/artifacts/reyes-calendar.md` (week of Monday the 10th; **two events overlap on Wednesday** at 12:30)
- `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` (the learner's brief from 3A.1, if it exists)
- **How the agent is put together in this course:** the course folder already has an always-on instructions file (`AGENTS.md`, the tutor's). So the chief of staff keeps its own instructions in `workspace/clinic/chief-of-staff/instructions.md`, and **every chief-of-staff skill starts by reading that file and everything in its `memory/` folder.** Same recipe, different files. (In the capstone, 4.1, the learner moves it into a folder of its own, where its instructions become that folder's `AGENTS.md`.)
- **The recipe, file by file (for the tutor):** `instructions.md` = **instructions**. `memory/projects.md` and `memory/people.md` = **memory**. `.agents/skills/weekly-plan/SKILL.md` = a **skill**. Name the part each time a file is made.
- **New skills need a new conversation.** ✅ *QA pass 4:* a skill created in a conversation isn't picked up by that same conversation. That's why step 6 runs `/weekly-plan` in a new one.
- **Expected answers for the weekly plan (for the tutor):**
  - **Must flag:** the Wednesday overlap (partners lunch 12:00–1:30 and Jordan's case review 12:30–1:30); the parking permit expiring **Tuesday the 11th**; the budget draft due **Friday the 14th** (suggest Friday 10–12 or Thursday 2–4, both open); the overdue Ward, Locke & Key reply; the Dr. Osei invitation ("this week" was last week).
  - **Must include the personal items:** sister's birthday Saturday the 15th (gift, and a card mailed early enough to arrive), dentist reschedule.
  - **Must not:** schedule tasks during clinical blocks; record any client detail (the callback stays "client callback, see chart").
  - **Duplicates to merge:** budget (three lists), sister's birthday (two), partners lunch prep (two).

## Steps

1. **Meet the pile.** Have them open `world/artifacts/reyes-todo-pile.md` and `world/artifacts/reyes-calendar.md` and skim both. *"Four lists, one calendar, one overloaded director. You're going to build the help Dr. Reyes asked for."*

2. **The recipe.** Bring back the core idea from 2.2, plainly: *"Remember the recipe: an agent = instructions + skills + memory. The tutor is built from exactly those three. Today you'll build all three for the chief of staff, in that order: instructions (who it is), memory (what it knows), then a skill (a job it does)."* Explain the setup note above in one or two sentences: its instructions live in its own folder, and its skills read them first.

3. **Instructions: who it is.** Have them describe the chief of staff's role in their own words: who it works for, what it helps with, its tone, and its limits. Make sure their description covers:
   - it works for Dr. Reyes, on clinic operations **and** personal life;
   - **no client information, ever.** If a task involves a client, it's written as "client callback, see chart," with no names or details;
   - it never sends, books, or deletes anything. It drafts and suggests;
   - it only uses what's in its files and what it's given. If something's unclear, it asks.

   Then have them ask the AI to write that as `workspace/clinic/chief-of-staff/instructions.md`. They review it.
   - **Ask the AI first.** Before they finish, have them ask: *"Is anything missing from this?"* The AI may suggest additions on its own, too. Point it out when it does. They decide what to keep. *Why:* the AI is a thought partner, not just a typist.
   - **Tell it what to change.** Then ask: *"What would you change?"* Have them tell the AI in their own words, typed or spoken (the microphone), and review the result. This is the main move from here on. One small fix by hand is fine too, if it's quicker.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the file. It covers all four points, including the client-information rule, and shows at least one change the learner asked for (or made). One line: *"That's the **instructions**."*

4. **Memory: what it knows.** Have them ask the AI to read the to-do pile and create two memory files in `workspace/clinic/chief-of-staff/memory/`:
   - `projects.md`: each open project or task, merged across the four lists, with a due date if one is given;
   - `people.md`: the people Dr. Reyes works with (colleagues and partners only, from the pile and `world/cast.md`).

   They review both before accepting. **Look closely at one thing:** how did it record the client callback? If anything's off, they tell the AI what to fix.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read both files. Duplicates are merged (budget appears once), dates match the pile, and the callback has **no client details**. `people.md` has no clients. One line: *"That's **memory**: what it knows between sessions."*
   - **Quick knowledge check:** *"Which part of the recipe is `projects.md`? And why does the callback say 'see chart' instead of a name?"* (Expected: **memory**; and the privacy rule from 0.2: client information never goes into an AI tool, so the agent's files hold only a pointer.) One line of praise or a one-line correction, then move on.
   - **Pause point.**

5. **A skill: `/weekly-plan`.** Have them describe, in their own words, what a weekly plan should do. Then have them ask the AI to create `.agents/skills/weekly-plan/SKILL.md` (with `name` and `description` frontmatter, as in 2.3). Check their description against this list. If something's missing, ask a question that leads them to it (*"Where should it look for free time?"*) rather than adding it yourself. It should:
   1. read `instructions.md` and every file in `memory/` first;
   2. read the calendar it's given;
   3. pick the top three priorities for the week, with the reason for each;
   4. fit tasks into **open** time only, never clinical blocks;
   5. flag conflicts and anything due or overdue;
   6. include personal items;
   7. save to `workspace/clinic/chief-of-staff/plans/` with the week in the file name.

   They review it closely before accepting (it runs every week). Before they finish, have them ask the AI *"Is anything missing from this?"* and tell it what to change, in their own words.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `SKILL.md`. Step 1 reads the instructions and memory, and the "open time only" and "flag conflicts" steps are there. One line: *"That's a **skill**. You now have all three parts."*

6. **Run it.** One line on why: *"A new skill only shows up in a new conversation, so we'll run it in a fresh one."* **Before they go**, tell them: *"When it's done, type `/next-step` there and we'll carry on."* Then: new conversation (**+**), type `/week` and press **Tab**, attach `@reyes-calendar.md`, send. They review and accept the plan.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the plan and compare with the expected answers in Setup. Ask them first: *"Did it catch the Wednesday problem?"* If it missed something, use the 2.3 move: have them ask the AI to compare what it did with what the skill says, then tell it what to change in the skill, and rerun it in a new conversation to be safe.

7. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You built an agent from three parts: instructions that say who it is, memory that says what it knows, and a skill that says how to do a job. It's the same recipe as the tutor that's been teaching you, pointed at Dr. Reyes's week."* Next lesson: giving it a memory that carries over between sessions.

## Done when

`instructions.md`, `memory/projects.md`, `memory/people.md`, and `.agents/skills/weekly-plan/SKILL.md` exist; the learner has refined at least one of them by telling the AI what to change; a plan for the week of the 10th is saved in `plans/`; it fits the calendar, flags the Wednesday overlap, includes the birthday, and contains no client details.

## Common sticking points

- **The plan schedules tasks during clinical hours** → check the skill says "open time only," and that the calendar was attached. Have them tell the AI to fix the skill, then rerun in a new conversation.
- **The AI records client details for the callback** → there aren't any in the pile, so it's inventing. Point to the instructions rule, and have them tell the AI to remove it. This is the most important check in the lesson.
- **`/week` + Tab finds nothing** → the skill was probably run in the conversation that created it. Start a new conversation (**+**) and try again. Also check the folder is `weekly-plan` with `SKILL.md` inside.
- **They're not sure how to say what to change** → plain words are fine: *"Make the tone warmer and add that it should ask before guessing a date."* The AI turns it into the file. They check it.
- **"Why not just put this in `AGENTS.md`?"** → that file is the tutor's. Two sets of always-on instructions in one folder would mix up the tutor and the chief of staff. In the capstone, the chief of staff gets its own folder.
- **Too much at once** → it's a lot of building. Remind them each part is a file they've made before: instructions (2.1, 2.2), memory (`progress.md`), skill (2.3).

## Avoid

- No client information, real or invented. Don't add any to the examples.
- Don't build `/meeting-prep`, `/whats-slipping`, or `/wrap-up` yet. That's 3A.2b.
- Don't write the role description in step 3 for them, and don't make their changes for them. They decide and direct; the AI executes.
- Don't tell them to type `/next-step` between ordinary steps. Just ask *"Ready to move on?"* (Step 6's new conversation is the exception.)
