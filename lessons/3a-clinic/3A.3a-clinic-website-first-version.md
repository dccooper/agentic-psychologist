# Lesson 3A.3a: Vibe Code a Clinic Website: First Version

- **Module:** 3A (clinic)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~6 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner turns the clinic's 2017 website into a **single-page website** (one HTML file), built only from checked facts, and sees it in their browser. They describe it; the AI writes the code; they check it.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Referrals depend on people finding accurate information. Most clinicians can't build a website, and many can't afford someone who can. "Vibe coding" (describing what you want and letting AI write the code) puts a simple, accurate site within reach. The clinician's real job is the part AI can't do: knowing what's true and what's appropriate to say.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/daybreak-old-website.md` (the 2017 site: wrong hours, a retired phone number, an evening group that ended, a client testimonial, and a "proven to cure" claim)
- `world/artifacts/daybreak-facts.md` (Priya's checked facts: **the only source for the new site**)
- `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` (from 3A.1: the learner's own "done" for the website, if it exists)
- **Expected problems in the old site (for the tutor):** Saturday hours (closed weekends); old phone (555) 010-0199 (new: **(555) 010-0142**); "NEW evening group" (there is none); the client testimonial; "BEST clinic" and "proven to cure anxiety and depression"; "some testing" (it should name **assessments**); no mention that services are by **supervised trainees**; "Under construction."
- **Technical constraints for the page:** one file, `workspace/clinic/website/index.html`, with the styling inside it. **No frameworks, no outside scripts, fonts, or trackers.** It should open by double-clicking, with no internet. *Why no outside scripts:* a clinic website shouldn't quietly send visitors' data to anyone.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: reviewing a new file; **opening a file in the web browser** (⚠️ unverified in Antigravity: right-click the file in the Explorer → **Reveal in File Explorer** (Windows) / **Reveal in Finder** (Mac), then double-click it). If that option isn't there, the AI can open it with a command (explain the command first, per 2.4).

## Steps

1. **What's wrong with it?** Have them open `world/artifacts/daybreak-old-website.md` and `world/artifacts/daybreak-facts.md` side by side. Ask them to find **three things** on the old site that are wrong or shouldn't be there. Let them look first.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they find at least three items from the expected list in Setup. If they spot the testimonial or the "cure" claim, say: *"Hold that thought. Next lesson has an ethics check."*

2. **What does done look like?** If `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` exists, have them open it and reread their "done" for the website (they wrote it in 3A.1). If not, ask: *"How should it look and feel, and what should a visitor see first?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they can say, in a sentence or two, how they want it to look and what comes first.

3. **Think first: a page plan.** Have them ask the AI for a **plan of the page** in chat (no file yet): the sections, in order, and one line on what each says. Based on `daybreak-facts.md` and their "done." Then they adjust it (move a section, cut one, rename one). *Why:* read, think, act from 1.2. Changing a plan is cheaper than changing a page.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've agreed a section list and changed at least one thing.

4. **Act: build it.** Have them ask the AI, using the four parts from 1.3, to build the page at `workspace/clinic/website/index.html`: their plan, their look and feel, **facts only from `@daybreak-facts.md`**, and the technical constraints in Setup (one file, no outside scripts or fonts). Write it.
   - They review it before accepting, but it's code, so they won't read every line. **That's okay.** Have them glance at it and notice the parts they recognize (the clinic's name, the phone number). The real check is the browser, next.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/clinic/website/index.html` exists. **Pause point.**

5. **See it.** Have them open it in their web browser (see UI above). *"That's a real website, made by describing it."* Give them a moment with it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they can see the page in a browser.

6. **Check the facts.** Have them check the page against `daybreak-facts.md` themselves, one item at a time: hours, phone number, assessments named, sliding scale, **"provided by doctoral trainees under the supervision of licensed psychologists,"** the crisis line. Anything wrong, they ask the AI to fix, then **refresh the browser** to see the change.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `index.html` and confirm: phone **(555) 010-0142**, weekdays only, assessments named, trainee/supervision statement present, no evening group, no testimonial, no outside scripts or fonts (no `http` links to code).

7. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You made a working website without writing code, and you checked every fact on it yourself. The AI wrote it, and you're the reason it's true."* Next lesson: refining it, and an ethics and accessibility check.

## Pause points

- After step 4 (the page exists).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/clinic/website/index.html` exists, opens in a browser, follows their plan, and every fact on it matches `daybreak-facts.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **They can't open it in the browser** → try the Reveal option in the Explorer's right-click menu, then double-click the file. Or have the AI open it with a command (it should explain the command first). Note what worked in `progress.md` for the course authors.
- **The page loads a font or script from the internet** → ask the AI to remove it and use built-in fonts. A clinic site shouldn't call out to other services.
- **The AI keeps something from the old site** (the testimonial, Saturday hours) → it's a good catch. The facts file is the only source. Old copy isn't a source.
- **"I can't read the code"** → you don't need to. You described it, you'll check it in the browser, and next lesson the AI explains it.
- **They want to publish it** → that's Graduation (G.2). For now it lives on their computer.

## Avoid

- No client stories, quotes, or details on the page, not even invented ones.
- No frameworks, build tools, or installs.
- Don't do the fact check in step 6 for them.
