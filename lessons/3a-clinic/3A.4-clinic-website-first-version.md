# Lesson 3A.4: Vibe Code a Clinic Website: First Version

- **Module:** 3A (clinic)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~7 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 4: passed. Revised from the tester's notes; retest in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner turns the clinic's 2017 website into a **single-page website** (one HTML file), built only from checked facts and shaped by examples of what they think good looks like, and sees it in their browser. They describe it; the AI writes the code; they check it.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Referrals depend on people finding accurate information. Most clinicians can't build a website, and many can't afford someone who can. "Vibe coding" (describing what you want and letting AI write the code) puts a simple, accurate site within reach. The clinician's real job is the part AI can't do: knowing what's true, what's appropriate to say, and what good looks like.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/daybreak-old-website.md` (the 2017 site: wrong hours, a retired phone number, an evening group that ended, a client testimonial, and a "proven to cure" claim)
- `world/artifacts/daybreak-facts.md` (Priya's checked facts: **the only source for the new site**)
- `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` (from 3A.1: the learner's own "done" for the website, if it exists)
- **Expected problems in the old site (for the professor):** Saturday hours (closed weekends); old phone (555) 010-0199 (new: **(555) 010-0142**); "NEW evening group" (there is none); the client testimonial; "BEST clinic" and "proven to cure anxiety and depression"; "some testing" (it should name **assessments**); no mention that services are by **supervised trainees**; "Under construction."
- **Technical constraints for the page:** one file, `workspace/clinic/website/index.html`, with the styling inside it. **No frameworks, no outside scripts, fonts, or trackers.** It should open straight from the file, with no internet. *Why no outside scripts:* a clinic website shouldn't quietly send visitors' data to anyone.
- **Taste, borrowed not copied:** in step 3 the learner brings an example site. Use it for the *feel* (layout, colors, tone, how quickly you find the phone number). **Never copy another site's text, logos, images, or branding.**
- **Scaffold lightly.** This is late in the path. Ask *"What would you change?"* before offering options.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: reviewing a new file; the **microphone** (optional).

- **Opening the page in the browser** ⚠️ *unverified in Antigravity.* **Main way:** the learner asks the AI to open it. Before running anything, the AI explains in one short line: *"This opens your page in your default web browser. It doesn't change anything. A box will ask you to allow it."* Then it runs:
  - Windows (PowerShell): `Start-Process "workspace/clinic/website/index.html"` (or `start` with the same path)
  - Mac: `open workspace/clinic/website/index.html`

  The learner approves once. After later edits, they just **refresh the browser tab**.
- **Fallback:** right-click `index.html` in the Explorer → **Reveal in File Explorer** (Windows) / **Reveal in Finder** (Mac), then double-click the file.
- ⚠️ *Possibly:* Antigravity has its own built-in browser that the agent can use to look at pages it edits. Unconfirmed for local files. Don't depend on it.
- **Opening a URL** ⚠️ *unverified:* whether the agent can open a web address the learner gives it (step 3). If it can't, the learner describes the site or pastes a screenshot.

## Steps

1. **What's wrong with it?** Have them open `world/artifacts/daybreak-old-website.md` and `world/artifacts/daybreak-facts.md` side by side. Ask them to find **three things** on the old site that are wrong or shouldn't be there. Let them look first.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they find at least three items from the expected list in Setup. If they spot the testimonial or the "cure" claim, say: *"Hold that thought. Next lesson has an ethics check."*

2. **What does done look like?** If `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` exists, have them open it and reread their "done" for the website (they wrote it in 3A.1). If not, ask: *"How should it look and feel, and what should a visitor see first?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they can say, in a sentence or two, how they want it to look and what comes first.

3. **What good looks like.** Say it plainly: *"You know what good looks like. The AI doesn't, until you show it. The more examples of 'good' you give it before it starts, the closer the first version lands."* Ask them for:
   - **one website they like**, or want this to resemble (any site: another clinic, a favorite organization, a local business). The web address is enough;
   - **what specifically they like about it:** the layout, the colors, the tone, the photos, how fast you find the phone number;
   - **any design changes** they already want, in their own words.

   If the AI can't open the address, they describe the site or paste a screenshot instead. One line of caution: *"Borrow the feel, never the words, logos, or branding."*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've named an example and at least two specific things they like about it.

4. **Think first: a page plan.** Have them ask the AI for a **plan of the page** in chat (no file yet): the sections, in order, one line on what each says, and a short **look and feel** note built from their example and likes. Based on `daybreak-facts.md`, their "done," and step 3. Then ask: *"What would you change?"* They tell the AI (move a section, cut one, rename one, adjust the look). Have them also ask: *"Is anything missing from this plan?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've agreed a section list with their look-and-feel notes in it, and changed at least one thing.
   - **Quick knowledge check:** *"Which step of read, think, act is this plan? And why do it before building?"* (Expected: **think**; changing a plan is cheaper than changing a page.)

5. **Act: build it.** Have them ask the AI, using the four parts from 1.3, to build the page at `workspace/clinic/website/index.html`: their plan and look-and-feel notes, **facts only from `@daybreak-facts.md`**, and the technical constraints in Setup (one file, no outside scripts or fonts). Write it.
   - They review it before accepting, but it's code, so they won't read every line. **That's okay.** Have them glance at it and notice the parts they recognize (the clinic's name, the phone number). The real check is the browser, next.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/clinic/website/index.html` exists.
   - **Pause point.**

6. **See it.** Have them ask the AI to open the page in their browser (see UI above). The AI explains the command in one line first, then runs it, and they approve. *"That's a real website, made by describing it."* Give them a moment with it. Ask: *"How close is it to the site you liked?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they can see the page in a browser. If the command didn't work, use the Reveal fallback.

7. **Check the facts.** Have them check the page against `daybreak-facts.md` themselves, one item at a time: hours, phone number, assessments named, sliding scale, **"provided by doctoral trainees under the supervision of licensed psychologists,"** the crisis line. Anything wrong, they tell the AI to fix it, then **refresh the browser tab** to see the change.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `index.html` and confirm: phone **(555) 010-0142**, weekdays only, assessments named, trainee/supervision statement present, no evening group, no testimonial, no outside scripts or fonts (no `http` links to code), and no text, logo, or images copied from their example site.

8. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You made a working website without writing code: you showed it what good looks like, and you checked every fact on it yourself. The AI wrote it, and you're the reason it's true."* Next lesson: refining it, and an ethics and accessibility check.

## Pause points

- After step 5 (the page exists).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/clinic/website/index.html` exists, opens in a browser, follows their plan and look-and-feel notes, and every fact on it matches `daybreak-facts.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **The browser doesn't open** → check the AI explained and ran the command, and that they allowed it. Then try the fallback: right-click `index.html` in the Explorer → **Reveal in File Explorer** / **Reveal in Finder**, then double-click. Note what worked in `progress.md` for the course authors.
- **They can't think of a site they like** → any site counts, not just clinics: a library, a café, a charity. Or: *"What's a website where you found what you needed fast?"*
- **The AI can't open the web address** → they describe it in their own words, or paste a screenshot. Both work.
- **The page copies wording or a logo from their example** → have them tell the AI to remove it. Borrow the feel, never the content.
- **The page loads a font or script from the internet** → have them tell the AI to remove it and use built-in fonts. A clinic site shouldn't call out to other services.
- **The AI keeps something from the old site** (the testimonial, Saturday hours) → it's a good catch. The facts file is the only source. Old copy isn't a source.
- **"I can't read the code"** → you don't need to. You described it, you'll check it in the browser, and next lesson the AI explains it.
- **They want to publish it** → that's Graduation (G.2). For now it lives on their computer.

## Avoid

- No client stories, quotes, or details on the page, not even invented ones.
- No frameworks, build tools, or installs.
- Don't copy anything from the learner's example site except the general feel.
- Don't run the open-in-browser command without explaining it first.
- Don't do the fact check in step 7 for them.
- Don't tell them to type `/next-step` between steps. Just ask *"Ready to move on?"*
