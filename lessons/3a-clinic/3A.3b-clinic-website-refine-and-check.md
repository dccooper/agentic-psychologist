# Lesson 3A.3b: Vibe Code a Clinic Website: Refine and Check

- **Module:** 3A (clinic)
- **Time:** ~20 min
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner refines the site in a **describe → build → look** loop, has the AI explain a piece of the code and edits it by hand, then runs three checks: **professional ethics** (advertising), **accessibility**, and **who the page is for**.

## Why it matters to a psychologist

A clinic website is a public statement by psychologists, so the profession's ethics rules apply to it. AI writes marketing copy fluently, including the kinds of claims and testimonials psychologists aren't supposed to use. Knowing what to check for is professional judgment that AI doesn't replace.

## Setup

- `workspace/clinic/website/index.html` (from 3A.3a). **If it's missing**, go back to 3A.3a.
- `world/artifacts/daybreak-facts.md` and `world/artifacts/referral-partners.md` (for the audience check: the Community Health Collective serves many Spanish-speaking families)
- **The ethics standard (US):** the APA *Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct*, **Standard 5, Advertising and Other Public Statements**, especially **5.01** (no false, deceptive, or misleading statements, including about training and credentials) and **5.05** (psychologists don't solicit testimonials from current therapy clients, or from others vulnerable to undue influence; Daybreak's facts sheet rules out testimonials entirely). The full code is free on apa.org. **For educators outside the US:** swap in your own code (e.g., the BPS, CPA, or APS code), and keep the same exercise.
- **Expected ethics findings (for the tutor):** no superlatives ("best"), no outcome guarantees ("proven to cure"), no testimonials, trainee and supervision status stated clearly, and nothing that implies services the facts sheet doesn't list (e.g., crisis care).

## Steps

1. **The loop.** Have them open the page in the browser again and pick **two changes** they want (layout, colors, wording, order). For each one: describe it to the AI, review the change, **refresh the browser**, and look. *Why:* describe → build → look is the whole of vibe coding. Small steps are easier to check.
   - *Optional:* if they can paste a screenshot into the agent panel, they can point at what they mean. Note whether it works in `progress.md`.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** two changes made and seen in the browser.

2. **Look under the hood.** Have them ask the AI to explain, in plain language, how **one part** of the page works (the header, or where the colors are set). Then have them change one small thing **by hand** in `index.html`, such as a color or a heading's wording, save, and refresh. *Why:* the code isn't magic either. It's text you can read and edit, like `AGENTS.md`.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they made a hand edit to the code and saw it in the browser. **Pause point.**

3. **Ethics check.** Explain in two sentences that a clinic website is a public statement, so APA Ethics Code Standard 5 applies (see Setup; they can read it on apa.org). Have them ask the AI to check the page against **5.01** and **5.05** and against the "not to be included" line in `daybreak-facts.md`, and **list** any problems (not fix them yet). Then they decide what to change, and ask for those fixes.
   - **Say plainly:** the AI's check is a first pass, not a ruling. They're the professional. For a real practice, check your licensing board's rules too.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `index.html` and compare with the expected findings in Setup. In particular: no testimonials, no "best" or "cure," and the trainee/supervision statement is clear.

4. **Accessibility check.** Have them ask the AI to check the page for basic accessibility: headings in order, **alt text** on any images, enough **color contrast**, readable text size, clear link text, and whether it works on a phone screen. Then have them **narrow the browser window** to phone width and look. Fix what they agree with.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they fixed at least one accessibility issue and checked the narrow view.

5. **Who is this for?** Ask: *"Who does this page assume it's talking to? Who might read it and feel it isn't for them?"* Let them think first. Prompts if needed: someone who's never heard "psychological assessment," a parent worried about cost, a teenager, a Spanish-speaking family referred by the Community Health Collective. Have them make **one change** for a reader they'd missed.
   - If they add Spanish text: it must match the facts ("available on some days; call to ask"), and **AI translations should be checked by a fluent speaker** before going live.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they named a reader the page didn't serve well and made one change for them.

6. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You built a website by describing it, and then did the part only a psychologist could: checked it against your profession's ethics, made it usable for more people, and asked who it leaves out. Building is fast now; judgment is the job."* Publishing it comes in Graduation (G.2).

## Done when

The page has two learner-chosen refinements and a hand edit; it passes the ethics check (no testimonials, no guarantees or superlatives, trainee status clear); at least one accessibility fix is made; and one change serves a reader they'd missed.

## Common sticking points

- **The AI adds new marketing claims while fixing something else** ("compassionate, evidence-based excellence") → check every change, not just the one you asked for. It's 1.1's review habit.
- **They disagree with the AI's ethics read** → good. Talk it through. Their judgment wins. The Ethics Code itself is free to read on apa.org.
- **The page looks broken after a hand edit** → Ctrl+Z, save, refresh. Or ask the AI what went wrong. Breaking it and fixing it is how people learn code.
- **They're not in the US** → use their own code of ethics. The exercise is the same.

## Avoid

- Don't add client stories or quotes, even as "examples" of what not to do.
- Don't present the AI's ethics check as authoritative.
- Don't publish anything. Don't install anything.
