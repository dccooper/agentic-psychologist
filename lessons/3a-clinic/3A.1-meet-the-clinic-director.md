# Lesson 3A.1: Meet the Clinic Director

- **Module:** 3A (clinic)
- **Time:** ~20 min (added the `/tidy-up` skill)
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 3: passed. Step 7 (`/tidy-up`) added after QA pass 4; retest in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner turns a rambling request into **two scoped projects** written in a short brief, before building anything: **scope before you build.**

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Clinic directors, supervisors, and colleagues ask for help in exactly this form: a long, kind, unfocused voice memo. Turning that into "here's what I'll do, here's what I won't, here's what done looks like" is a professional skill. AI makes it faster, but only if you scope first.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/reyes-voice-memo.md` (Dr. Reyes's voice memo; contains a callback to the 0.2 rule: Priya stopped them pasting the waitlist)
- `world/clinic.md` (background on the Daybreak Clinic)
- The learner chose the **Clinic** path (recorded in `progress.md`).

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: opening files; reviewing and accepting a new file. Uses the "ask it for a plan, not an action" move from Lesson 1.2.

## Steps

1. **Welcome to the clinic.** Two sentences: they're on the Clinic path now, at the Daybreak Clinic. Everything on this path is about running the *business* of a clinic, and **none of it uses client information**. Have them open `world/clinic.md` and skim it.

2. **The voice memo.** Have them open `world/artifacts/reyes-voice-memo.md` and read it. It rambles on purpose.

3. **Spot the privacy moment.** Ask: *"Did you notice the moment in the memo that connects to Lesson 0.2?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they find it. (Expected answer: Dr. Reyes almost pasted the **waitlist** into an AI tool, and Priya stopped them. Waitlists hold client names and contact details, so it's protected information.) One line of praise, then move on.

4. **What does Dr. Reyes actually want?** Have them ask the AI, in their own words, to pull out what Dr. Reyes is actually asking for. Answer briefly, from the memo only.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the answer surfaces the **two projects**: (1) a **chief of staff** to keep Dr. Reyes organized, and (2) the **website / referral one-pager**, so local providers know what the clinic offers (including assessments). Plus the underlying problems: forgetting follow-ups, an outdated website, referrals down.

5. **Scope before you build.** Have them ask the AI for a **one-page project brief** saved to `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md`. For each of the two projects it should cover: *the problem*, *what we'll build*, *what we won't do* (including "no client information"), and *what "done" looks like*. *Why:* a brief is the plan from 1.2, applied to a real request. It keeps both you and the AI from building the wrong thing.
   - Write it (this creates the `workspace/clinic/` folder too). They review and accept it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` exists, with both projects.

6. **Make it theirs.** Ask them to read the brief and change at least one thing. Suggest the easiest place to start: **rewrite what "done" looks like in their own words.** For the website, that might be how they want it to look and feel (warm or clinical, colors, photos or none), what a visitor should see first, or what a local provider should be able to find in ten seconds. For the chief of staff, what a good Monday morning looks like for Dr. Reyes. A "won't do" they'd add works too. *Why:* "done" is where the brief becomes *their* project, and it's what they'll check the AI's work against when they build it. Then have them check the brief against the memo: did the AI invent anything Dr. Reyes didn't say?
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they made an edit, and they've checked it against the memo.

7. **A skill to keep things tidy: `/tidy-up`.** Have them look at the Explorer: `world/artifacts/` and their own `workspace/` are filling up, and they'll keep growing on this path. *"Instead of tidying by hand every week, build a skill that does it, and run it whenever things feel messy."* Have them describe what it should do, then ask the AI to create `.agents/skills/tidy-up/SKILL.md` (with `name` and `description` frontmatter, as in 2.3). Make sure it covers:
   1. **Read, think, act (1.2):** look first, then write a short tidy plan, then wait for their OK before changing anything.
   2. **An index:** keep `workspace/INDEX.md` up to date, a one-line description of every file in `world/artifacts/` and `workspace/`, grouped by who it's from or what it's for.
   3. **Move only their own files**, in `workspace/`. **Never move, rename, or delete anything in `world/`, `data/`, `lessons/`, or `.agents/`:** lessons and skills point to those files by name. (That's why the department's shared drive gets an index, not a reorganization.)
   4. **Before moving a file, check whether anything points to it** (a skill, a rule, an agent's instructions). If something does, leave it, or update the pointer too.
   5. **Never delete.** Old drafts go to `workspace/archive/`.

   Before they finish: *"Ask it: is anything missing from this?"* Then **new conversation** (**+**; a new skill only shows up there), `/tidy` + **Tab**. **Before they go:** *"When it's done, type `/next-step` there."* They read the plan, approve or change it, and review the result.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/INDEX.md` exists and lists files from both folders, and nothing in `world/` moved. **Knowledge check:** *"Why can't `/tidy-up` reorganize `world/artifacts/`?"* (Expected: lessons and skills point to those files by name, so moving them would break things. Checking what points to a file before moving it is a real-world habit too.)
   - One line: *"Run `/tidy-up` any time things feel messy. I'll remind you now and then."*

8. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You turned a rambling request into a clear brief before building anything. Scoping first is how you make sure the AI's speed goes toward the right thing."* Then tell them what's next: the next lessons build the chief of staff, then the website. Tie it to 2.2 in one line: *"The chief of staff will be an agent built from the same recipe as your tutor: instructions + skills + memory."*

## Pause points

- After step 4 (they know what Dr. Reyes wants): a safe break.
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/clinic/project-brief.md` exists, covers both projects with problem / build / won't do / done, has at least one learner edit, and the learner has checked it against the memo. `/tidy-up` exists and has run once, producing `workspace/INDEX.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **They don't spot the privacy moment** → hint: "What did Priya stop Dr. Reyes from doing?"
- **The AI's brief promises things the memo never asked for** (e.g., "a patient portal") → teaching moment. Check it against the source, and cut it.
- **They want to start building right away** → good instinct, but the brief comes first. The building starts in the next lesson.

## Avoid

- No client information, even made-up client details. The projects are about operations and outreach.
- Don't build the chief of staff or the website in this lesson.
- Don't let `/tidy-up` move anything outside `workspace/`.
- Don't write the "won't do" or "done" lines for them in step 6. The edit is theirs.
