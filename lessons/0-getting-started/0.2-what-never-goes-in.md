# Lesson 0.2: What Never Goes into an AI Tool

- **Module:** 0 (shared)
- **Time:** ~15 min
- **Request budget:** ~6 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity IDE 2.5.5, 2026-10-24, QA pass 2 (David). Passed, no bugs.

## Goal

The learner can explain, in their own words, why real patient and participant data never goes into an AI tool without institutional approval, and can spot the common traps (especially "I removed the name").

## Why it matters to a psychologist

This is the mistake that can end a career, harm a client, or shut down a study. Curiosity after a lesson that works is exactly when people slip.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/can-i-paste-this.md`: a required data-handling exercise emailed by the Office of Research Compliance (Ward, Locke & Key), with eight scenarios. The email gives no answers. Keep it that way.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: opening a file in the Explorer; reviewing the change when you save `my-rule.md` (⚠️ review UI not yet verified).

## Steps

1. **Frame it.** Two sentences: AI tools send what you type to a company's servers to be processed. Unless your institution has approved the tool, with the right agreements in place (for clinical data in the U.S., usually a Business Associate Agreement under HIPAA), that's disclosing confidential information.

2. **Open the compliance email.** Every new student gets this one. Have them open `world/artifacts/can-i-paste-this.md` from the **Explorer**, the same way as in 0.1. (Practice for the skill they just learned.)
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** file is open.

3. **They answer first.** Ask them to reply in the chat with their answer for each item: OK, NOT OK, or IT DEPENDS. A quick format like "1 OK, 2 NOT OK…" is fine. **Don't reveal answers before they commit.**
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they've answered all eight.

4. **Debrief.** Go through *only the ones they missed or marked IT DEPENDS*, briefly, using the key below. Praise correct reasoning in one line. Keep it conversational, not a lecture.

5. **Their rule, in their words.** Ask them to write a one-sentence rule for themselves. Save it to `workspace/my-rule.md` **only after they write it**, using their exact words.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/my-rule.md` contains their rule.

6. **The guardrail.** One or two sentences: this course's professor is told to stop if it sees what looks like real patient data, **but that's a seatbelt, not a driver.** They are the real protection.

7. **Name the moment.** One or two sentences: *"The question isn't 'is AI safe?' It's 'is this information allowed to leave my hands, into this tool?' You just practiced asking that, and it works the same for any tool you ever use."*

## Pause points

- After step 3 (all eight answered): a safe break. The debrief can wait.
- End of lesson.

## Answer key (professor only)

| # | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **OK** | Fictional, no client information. *(In real life: internal emails can still be confidential, so follow your institution's policy.)* |
| 2 | **NOT OK** | **The big one.** Removing the name doesn't de-identify a note. Age, job, town, life events, dates and diagnoses together can identify someone. And the session content itself is protected. |
| 3 | **OK** | Your own information, your choice. Just know it's leaving your computer. |
| 4 | **NOT OK** | Names and appointment times together are protected health information, even without diagnoses. A screenshot is still data. |
| 5 | **OK** | Openly licensed public data. Check the license and any terms of use. |
| 6 | **IT DEPENDS (default NOT OK)** | ID numbers don't make it free to use. Use of research data is governed by the IRB protocol and consent forms. If the protocol doesn't approve this tool, don't. |
| 7 | **OK** | A public document written to be shared. |
| 8 | **NOT OK** | A "lightly disguised" real client is still a real client. A real composite or fictional case is fine. A disguised individual isn't. |

## Done when

All eight answered, the misses discussed, and their own rule saved to `workspace/my-rule.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **They mark #2 OK** → very common. Ask: "If a colleague read it, could they guess who it is? Could the client recognize themselves?"
- **They ask "then what CAN I use AI for in my work?"** → great question. Answer: lots, including everything in this course (planning, writing, marketing, learning, analysis of public data). The capstone covers how to responsibly take the next step with real work.
- **Legal anxiety spiral** → keep it practical: "When in doubt, leave it out, and ask your supervisor or compliance office."

## Avoid

- Don't give legal advice beyond the general principle. Point them to their institution.
- Don't make it scary for its own sake. The tone is *"here's how to stay safe and still get the benefits."*
- Don't write their rule for them.
