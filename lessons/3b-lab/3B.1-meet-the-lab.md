# Lesson 3B.1: Meet the Lab

- **Module:** 3B (lab)
- **Time:** ~20 min (added the `/tidy-up` skill)
- **Request budget:** ~8 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 3: passed. Step 7 (`/tidy-up`) added after QA pass 4; retest in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner turns a vague PI request into **scoped projects with a question each**, written in a short brief, before building anything: **scope before you build, and ask "what question does this answer?" first.**

## Why it matters to a psychologist

Research requests arrive vague ("quick lit review," "a dashboard like that paper"). The habit of pinning down the question first is the same habit that underlies good study design and preregistration. AI makes reading and analysis faster, so it makes this habit more important, not less.

## Setup

- `world/artifacts/lund-email-chain.md` (Dr. Lund's three asks, plus Dr. Bello's dry translation)
- `world/lab.md` (background on the Foresight Lab)
- The learner chose the **Lab** path (recorded in `progress.md`).
- **Firewall reminder (professor only):** don't search for, name, or summarize any specific published paper or its results in this lesson. The dataset and its paper come later, and the blind comparison in 3B.3b depends on it. Keep the lit-review topic general.

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: opening files; reviewing and accepting a new file. Uses the "ask it for a plan, not an action" move from Lesson 1.2.

## Steps

1. **Welcome to the lab.** Two sentences: they're on the Lab path now, at the Foresight Lab, which studies how people imagine and plan for the future. Everything on this path uses **openly licensed public data** only. Have them open `world/lab.md` and skim it.

2. **The email chain.** Have them open `world/artifacts/lund-email-chain.md` and read both emails.

3. **What's actually being asked?** Have them ask the AI, in their own words, to list Dr. Lund's requests and Dr. Bello's advice about each. Answer from the emails only.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the answer surfaces **three projects**: (1) a lit review on episodic future thinking and delay discounting, (2) a dashboard from the lab's dataset, (3) the grant's background section. And Dr. Bello's key advice: **ask what question each one answers, and decide the analysis question before looking at results.**

4. **Find the missing questions.** Ask them: *"Which of the three requests doesn't yet say what question it answers?"* Let them think first.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they see that the lit review and the dashboard have **no stated question**. The grant section depends on the lit review.

5. **Scope before you build.** Have them ask the AI for a **one-page project brief** saved to `workspace/lab/project-brief.md`. For each project it should cover: *the question it answers* (a draft is fine), *what we'll build*, *what we won't do*, and *what "done" looks like*. Have them include "decide the analysis question before seeing results" under the dashboard. *Why:* a brief is the plan from 1.2, applied to research. The question comes first.
   - Write it (this creates the `workspace/lab/` folder too). They review and accept it.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/lab/project-brief.md` exists, with a question for each project.

6. **Make it theirs.** Ask them to sharpen at least one question in the brief, in their own words. For example, narrow "what's known about future thinking" to something answerable. Then check: did the AI add any claims or citations that weren't in the emails?
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** they edited a question, and they've checked for invented claims or citations. If the AI cited any papers in the brief, flag them: *"Where did these come from? We'll deal with made-up citations properly in 3B.2a."*

7. **A skill to keep things tidy: `/tidy-up`.** Have them look at the Explorer: `world/artifacts/` and their own `workspace/` are filling up, and they'll keep growing on this path. *"Instead of tidying by hand every week, build a skill that does it, and run it whenever things feel messy."* Have them describe what it should do, then ask the AI to create `.agents/skills/tidy-up/SKILL.md` (with `name` and `description` frontmatter, as in 2.3). Make sure it covers:
   1. **Read, think, act (1.2):** look first, then write a short tidy plan, then wait for their OK before changing anything.
   2. **An index:** keep `workspace/INDEX.md` up to date, a one-line description of every file in `world/artifacts/` and `workspace/`, grouped by who it's from or what it's for.
   3. **Move only their own files**, in `workspace/`. **Never move, rename, or delete anything in `world/`, `data/`, `lessons/`, or `.agents/`:** lessons and skills point to those files by name. (That's why the department's shared drive gets an index, not a reorganization.)
   4. **Before moving a file, check whether anything points to it** (a skill, a rule, an agent's instructions). If something does, leave it, or update the pointer too.
   5. **Never delete.** Old drafts go to `workspace/archive/`.

   Before they finish: *"Ask it: is anything missing from this?"* Then **new conversation** (**+**; a new skill only shows up there), `/tidy` + **Tab**. **Before they go:** *"When it's done, type `/next-step` there."* They read the plan, approve or change it, and review the result.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `workspace/INDEX.md` exists and lists files from both folders, and nothing in `world/` moved. **Knowledge check:** *"Why can't `/tidy-up` reorganize `world/artifacts/`?"* (Expected: lessons and skills point to those files by name, so moving them would break things. Checking what points to a file before moving it is a real-world habit too.)
   - One line: *"Run `/tidy-up` any time things feel messy. I'll remind you now and then."*

8. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"You turned three vague requests into projects with questions before doing any reading or analysis. 'What question does this answer?' is the habit that keeps fast AI work honest."* Then tell them what's next: building a lab agent for the reading, then the analysis. Tie it to 2.2 in one line: *"The lab agent will be built from the same recipe as your professor: instructions + skills + memory."*

## Pause points

- After step 4 (they've found the missing questions): a safe break.
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`workspace/lab/project-brief.md` exists, covers all three projects with question / build / won't do / done, has at least one learner-sharpened question, and has been checked for invented claims. `/tidy-up` exists and has run once, producing `workspace/INDEX.md`.

## Common sticking points

- **They write "everything about future thinking" as the question** → ask what decision or section it feeds. ("The grant background needs to show what, exactly?")
- **The AI fills the brief with paper titles and findings** → teaching moment, and a firewall issue. Remove them. Citations come only from papers we give the agent (3B.2a).
- **They want to open the dataset** → it's in `data/`, but it waits until the analysis lessons (3B.3a), after the question and plan are written. That's on purpose. (`data/README.md` asks them to wait, too.)

## Avoid

- Don't name, search for, or summarize specific published papers or results (firewall for 3B.3b).
- Don't start the lit review or the analysis in this lesson.
- Don't let `/tidy-up` move anything outside `workspace/`, and keep `data/` untouched (the dataset waits for 3B.3a).
- Don't write the sharpened question for them in step 6.
