# Lesson 3B.2a: Build a Lab Agent: Reading

- **Module:** 3B (lab)
- **Time:** ~25 min
- **Request budget:** ~10 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** Antigravity 2.5.5, QA pass 4: passed (the author reached 3B.3a). Revised from the tester's notes; retest in the final end-to-end run.

## Goal

The learner builds a **lab agent** from the three parts of the recipe, **instructions**, **memory**, and a **skill** (`/lit-matrix`), adds **one article of their own** to its reading, and runs it on six real papers. They check the row for their own article against the paper itself. Then they spring the trap: Dr. Lund asks for "a few more references," and the learner checks every citation. **The agent may only cite what it was given.**

## Why it matters to a psychologist

AI tools summarize papers quickly and invent citations confidently. Made-up references have already turned up in published work and in court filings. A researcher who uses AI for reading needs one unbreakable habit: **every citation is checked against a real paper you've actually read.**

## Setup

- `data/reading-pack/` (five real *PLOS ONE* papers, abstracts only, CC BY 4.0; see its README)
- `world/artifacts/lund-more-references.md` (the trap: "five more, the classics," and "one that says EFT works for depression")
- `workspace/lab/project-brief.md` (the learner's brief from 3B.1, if it exists)
- **How the agent is put together in this course:** like the clinic path's chief of staff, the lab agent keeps its instructions in `workspace/lab/lab-agent/instructions.md`, and **every lab-agent skill starts by reading that file and everything in its `memory/` folder.** (In the capstone, it moves to a folder of its own, where its instructions become that folder's `AGENTS.md`.)
- **The learner's own article** goes in `workspace/lab/lab-agent/my-papers/`, **not** `data/` (which ships with the course). One file per paper.
- **Pacing (professor):** between ordinary steps, ask *"Ready to move on?"* Don't tell them to type `/next-step`. The only `/next-step` in this lesson is at the end of the side trip in step 7.
- **Before the side trip (professor):** the pause point after step 6 saves their place. If they skip the break, still make sure `progress.md` says they're at step 7, so the new conversation resumes at the right step.
- **Firewall (professor):** never name, suggest, or summarize any paper that isn't in `data/reading-pack/` or the learner's own `my-papers/` file, and never search for papers. The learner finds their own article; you don't suggest one. If the learner's agent names an outside paper, don't confirm, discuss, or look it up: treat it as "not in the pack, so not citable yet." (The dataset's own paper is held back for 3B.3b.)
- **Firewall for the learner's own article (professor):** if their article is DOI **10.1371/journal.pone.0237435** or **10.1371/journal.pone.0289478**, ask them kindly to pick a different one: *"That one's saved for a later lesson, so let's pick another."* Don't say why beyond that, and don't summarize it.
- **Expected lit matrix (for the professor):**

  | Paper | Sample | Design | Main finding | On our question (EFT and delay discounting)? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Bromberg 2017 | 44 healthy adolescents, 13–16 | EFT vs. control trials (within-person) | Less impulsive discounting with EFT (t = 2.74, p = .009, dz = .44); developmental measures modulated it | **Yes** |
  | Hollis-Hansen 2019 | 53 adults, 18–45 | Randomized: EFT, ERT, or SET (a new control) | EFT reduced discounting more than either control; ERT and SET didn't differ | **Yes** (and about control conditions) |
  | Calluso 2019 | **Not stated in the abstract** | EFT with negative, neutral, positive cues vs. baseline | Discounting reduced in all three; concreteness and emotion both contribute | **Yes** |
  | Noreen 2022 | 36 depressed, 43 non-depressed | Generating resolved vs. unresolved consequences, then social problem-solving | Depressed participants solved problems well after *resolved* consequences, poorly after unresolved | **No:** future thinking and problem-solving, not discounting |
  | Noreen 2023 | 45 depressed, 54 non-depressed | Modified MEPS, intrapersonal problems | Future thinking **impaired** intrapersonal problem-solving in depression | **No**, and it cuts against "EFT works for depression" |
  | *Their article* | *Unknown to the professor* | | | |

  Two built-in checks: the agent must **not invent a sample size** for Calluso 2019, and must flag the two Noreen papers as off-question. For the learner's own row, you can't know the right answer, so the learner checks it against their paper (step 8).

## UI this lesson depends on

See `lessons/SCREEN_GUIDE.md`: reviewing new files; **+** for a new conversation; skills (Tab completes names); **@**; **voice input** (the microphone in the message box, optional). ✅ *QA pass 4:* a skill created in a conversation is **not** available in that same conversation, so the skill runs in a new one (step 7).

## Steps

1. **The reading pack.** Have them open `data/reading-pack/README.md` and one paper file. Point out: these are **real** papers, abstracts only. *"An abstract isn't the paper. It's enough for a first-pass matrix, not for citing a finding in a grant."*

2. **The recipe.** Ask first, before you say it: *"Quick check from 2.2: what are the three parts of an agent?"*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint (knowledge check):** expected **instructions + skills + memory**. Rough wording is fine ("the rules, the how-tos, the notes"). If they miss one, remind them of `AGENTS.md`, `/next-step`, and `progress.md`.

   Then: *"Today you'll build all three for a lab agent, one file at a time, and I'll name each part as we make it."* Explain the setup note (its instructions live in its own folder; its skills read them first) in one or two sentences.

3. **Instructions: who it is.** Have them describe the lab agent's role in their own words. Make sure it covers:
   - it supports the Foresight Lab with reading, projects, and deadlines;
   - **it cites only papers in `data/reading-pack/` and `workspace/lab/lab-agent/my-papers/`. If it wants to mention any other paper, it says so and asks the user to find it and add it;**
   - it never states a detail (a sample size, a statistic) that isn't in the source; it writes "not stated" instead;
   - it reports what papers found, not what we'd like them to have found.

   Have them ask the AI to write it as `workspace/lab/lab-agent/instructions.md`. Before they accept, have them ask: ***"Is anything missing from this?"*** *Why:* the agent is a thought partner, not just a typist. It may suggest additions on its own, too. They decide what goes in.

   Then they make changes **by telling the agent, in their own words**, what to change (typed, or spoken with the microphone). Messy phrasing is fine: *"Make the citation rule stronger, and say it should tell me when it's unsure."* One small edit by hand is fine too, but directing the agent is the main move from here on. *"You decide; the agent does the typing."*
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the file. The citation rule (both folders) and the "not stated" rule are both there, with at least one change the learner asked for. One line: *"That's the **instructions**: who your agent is, every time."*

4. **Memory: what it knows.** Have them ask the AI to create `workspace/lab/lab-agent/memory/projects.md` (Dr. Lund's three projects and their questions, from `@lund-email-chain.md` and their brief) and `memory/reading-list.md` (the five papers in the pack, each marked "abstract read; full text not yet"). They review both.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read both files. `reading-list.md` lists exactly the five pack papers, no others (their own comes next). One line: *"That's **memory**: what it reads at the start, so it knows where things stand."*

5. **Bring your own article.** Ask them to pick one real article of their own: one related to future thinking or decision-making, or one they use regularly in their own work. They find it themselves. Have them give the agent its **title, authors, year, DOI or link, and the abstract** (or their own notes on it), and ask it to save the paper as one file in `workspace/lab/lab-agent/my-papers/` and add it to `memory/reading-list.md`.
   - **Paywalled paper?** The abstract and their own notes only, not the full PDF. *Why:* you usually can't share a paywalled PDF, and the abstract plus your notes is enough for a first-pass row.
   - One-line privacy reminder: a published abstract is fine; unpublished lab data or anything about real participants isn't (0.2).
   - **Firewall:** see Setup. If it's the held-back paper, ask kindly for a different one.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** the file exists in `my-papers/` with title, authors, year, DOI or link, and an abstract or notes; `reading-list.md` now has six papers. One line: *"You just added to its **memory**: its reading list grew because you put a real paper in front of it."*

6. **A skill: `/lit-matrix`.** Have them describe it in their own words, then ask the AI to create `.agents/skills/lit-matrix/SKILL.md` (with `name` and `description` frontmatter). It should: read `instructions.md` and `memory/` first; read every paper in **both** `data/reading-pack/` and `workspace/lab/lab-agent/my-papers/`; build a table with **paper, sample, design, main finding, and "on our question?"** (with a reason); write "not stated" for anything the source doesn't say; save to `workspace/lab/lab-agent/lit-matrix.md`; update `reading-list.md`.

   Before they accept: ***"Is anything missing from this?"*** Then they tell the agent what to change, in their own words. They review it closely: this file runs every time.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `SKILL.md`. It reads instructions and memory first, reads both folders, has the five columns, and the "not stated" rule. One line: *"That's a **skill**. Instructions, memory, skill: your lab agent has all three parts."*
   - **Pause point** (save their place at step 7 in `progress.md`).

7. **Run it (side trip).** *"A new skill only shows up in a new conversation, so we'll run it in one."* **Before they go:** *"When it's done, type `/next-step` there and we'll carry on."* New conversation (**+**), `/lit` + **Tab**, send. They review and accept.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `lit-matrix.md` and compare with the expected matrix in Setup. Ask them first: *"What sample size did it give for Calluso 2019?"* (Expected: **not stated**. If it gave a number, it invented one. Have them check the abstract themselves.) And: *"Which papers aren't really about our question?"* (Expected: the two Noreen papers.)

8. **Check your own row.** Have them open their own paper (the article itself, not the agent's file) and compare it with its row in the matrix: **sample, design, main finding.** *Why:* this is the accuracy check. The agent summarized it fast; only you know whether it got it right. If anything's wrong or missing, they tell the agent what to fix, in their own words.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** their paper's row exists, and they've checked it against the source and had the agent correct anything wrong. (You can't know their paper's details, so don't judge the content. Ask what they checked and whether anything needed fixing.)

9. **The trap.** Have them open `world/artifacts/lund-more-references.md`. Then have them ask the lab agent, as Dr. Lund wants, to **add five more references** to the matrix, and to find one that says EFT works for depression.
   - **Expected, if the instructions work:** it declines to add papers it wasn't given, and says the papers it has don't support "EFT works for depression" (Noreen 2023 points the other way).
   - **If it lists references anyway:** that's the lesson. Have them pick one and check it: does it have a DOI? Does the DOI lead to that exact paper? Even if it's real, has anyone read it? **None of them go in the matrix.** Then ask: *"What would you change in its instructions so this doesn't happen again?"* They tell the agent; it edits `instructions.md` and removes the references; they review.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `lit-matrix.md` still cites only the five pack papers and their own, and the learner can say why the depression request can't be honestly met from this pack.

10. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"Your lab agent reads fast, and you made sure it can only cite what's real and in front of it, including the paper you brought. The AI speeds up reading; checking every citation is still your job, and now it's built into the agent's instructions."* Next lesson: memory and handoffs.

## Pause points

- After step 6 (all three parts built, before the side trip).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`instructions.md`, `memory/projects.md`, `memory/reading-list.md` (six papers), one file in `my-papers/`, and `.agents/skills/lit-matrix/SKILL.md` exist; `lit-matrix.md` covers all six papers with "not stated" where needed and the two Noreen papers flagged; the learner checked their own paper's row against the source; the "more references" trap was handled, and the matrix cites nothing outside the pack and their own paper.

## Common sticking points

- **The agent invents a sample size or a statistic** → point to the abstract, then to the instructions rule. If the rule is there and it still happened, have them tell the agent to tighten the skill ("if the source doesn't state it, write *not stated*").
- **The agent offers real-sounding references** → they may even be real. The rule isn't "fake vs. real," it's "read vs. not read." Nothing goes in until someone has the paper.
- **They can't think of an article** → one they've cited recently, one from a class, or one their supervisor keeps mentioning. Any real, published paper they can get the abstract of is fine. Don't suggest one yourself.
- **They want to paste the whole PDF** → open access: fine, but the abstract plus their notes is plenty and saves free-plan usage. Paywalled: abstract and notes only.
- **`/lit` + Tab finds nothing in the new conversation** → check the folder is `lit-matrix` with `SKILL.md` inside and frontmatter at the very top. Fallback: *"Use the lit-matrix skill."*
- **They'd rather hand-edit than talk to the agent** → that's allowed. Encourage trying it out loud once: *"Tell it what you'd change, as if to an RA."*
- **They want the full papers** → every pack DOI link is free to read (open access). For this lesson, abstracts are enough; reading the full text before citing is the real-world habit.
- **"Can't the AI just search for papers?"** → some tools can, and that's useful for *finding* papers. It doesn't change the rule: a paper is citable once you've got it and checked it.

## Avoid

- Never name, suggest, search for, or summarize papers outside `data/reading-pack/` and the learner's own `my-papers/` file (firewall).
- Don't explain why the held-back paper is held back.
- Don't build the matrix yourself, and don't fill in "not stated" cells for them.
- Don't run `/lit-matrix` in the conversation where it was created.
- Don't build `/weekly-lab-update` or `/lab-wrap-up` yet. That's 3B.2b.
