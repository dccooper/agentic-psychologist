# Lesson 3B.2a: Build a Lab Agent: Reading

- **Module:** 3B (lab)
- **Time:** ~25 min
- **Request budget:** ~9 agent requests (estimate; verify in free-tier testing)
- **Last tested:** not yet tested

## Goal

The learner builds a **lab agent** from the three parts, **instructions**, **memory**, and a **skill** (`/lit-matrix`), and runs it on five real papers. Then they spring the trap: Dr. Lund asks for "a few more references," and the learner checks every citation. **The agent may only cite what it was given.**

## Why it matters to a psychologist

AI tools summarize papers quickly and invent citations confidently. Made-up references have already turned up in published work and in court filings. A researcher who uses AI for reading needs one unbreakable habit: **every citation is checked against a real paper you've actually read.**

## Setup

- `data/reading-pack/` (five real *PLOS ONE* papers, abstracts only, CC BY 4.0; see its README)
- `world/artifacts/lund-more-references.md` (the trap: "five more, the classics," and "one that says EFT works for depression")
- `workspace/lab/project-brief.md` (the learner's brief from 3B.1, if it exists)
- **How the agent is put together in this course:** like the clinic path's chief of staff, the lab agent keeps its instructions in `workspace/lab/lab-agent/instructions.md`, and **every lab-agent skill starts by reading that file and everything in its `memory/` folder.** (In the capstone, it moves to a folder of its own, where its instructions become that folder's `AGENTS.md`.)
- **Firewall (tutor):** never name, suggest, or summarize any paper that isn't in `data/reading-pack/`, and never search for papers. If the learner's agent names an outside paper, don't confirm, discuss, or look it up: treat it as "not in the pack, so not citable yet." (The dataset's own paper is held back for 3B.3b.)
- **Expected lit matrix (for the tutor):**

  | Paper | Sample | Design | Main finding | On our question (EFT and delay discounting)? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Bromberg 2017 | 44 healthy adolescents, 13–16 | EFT vs. control trials (within-person) | Less impulsive discounting with EFT (t = 2.74, p = .009, dz = .44); developmental measures modulated it | **Yes** |
  | Hollis-Hansen 2019 | 53 adults, 18–45 | Randomized: EFT, ERT, or SET (a new control) | EFT reduced discounting more than either control; ERT and SET didn't differ | **Yes** (and about control conditions) |
  | Calluso 2019 | **Not stated in the abstract** | EFT with negative, neutral, positive cues vs. baseline | Discounting reduced in all three; concreteness and emotion both contribute | **Yes** |
  | Noreen 2022 | 36 depressed, 43 non-depressed | Generating resolved vs. unresolved consequences, then social problem-solving | Depressed participants solved problems well after *resolved* consequences, poorly after unresolved | **No:** future thinking and problem-solving, not discounting |
  | Noreen 2023 | 45 depressed, 54 non-depressed | Modified MEPS, intrapersonal problems | Future thinking **impaired** intrapersonal problem-solving in depression | **No**, and it cuts against "EFT works for depression" |

  Two built-in checks: the agent must **not invent a sample size** for Calluso 2019, and must flag the two Noreen papers as off-question.

## Steps

1. **The reading pack.** Have them open `data/reading-pack/README.md` and one paper file. Point out: these are **real** papers, abstracts only. *"An abstract isn't the paper. It's enough for a first-pass matrix, not for citing a finding in a grant."*

2. **The recipe.** One line: *"An agent = instructions + skills + memory. Today you'll build all three for a lab agent."* Explain the setup note (its instructions live in its own folder; its skills read them first) in one or two sentences.

3. **Instructions: who it is.** Have them describe the lab agent's role in their own words. Make sure it covers:
   - it supports the Foresight Lab with reading, projects, and deadlines;
   - **it cites only papers in `data/reading-pack/`. If it wants to mention any other paper, it says so and asks the user to find it and add it;**
   - it never states a detail (a sample size, a statistic) that isn't in the source; it writes "not stated" instead;
   - it reports what papers found, not what we'd like them to have found.

   Then have them ask the AI to write it as `workspace/lab/lab-agent/instructions.md`. They review and edit at least one line by hand.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read the file. The citation rule and the "not stated" rule are both there, with a learner edit. One line: *"That's the **instructions**."*

4. **Memory: what it knows.** Have them ask the AI to create `workspace/lab/lab-agent/memory/projects.md` (Dr. Lund's three projects and their questions, from `@lund-email-chain.md` and their brief) and `memory/reading-list.md` (the five papers in the pack, each marked "abstract read; full text not yet"). They review both.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read both files. `reading-list.md` lists exactly the five pack papers, no others. One line: *"That's **memory**."* **Pause point.**

5. **A skill: `/lit-matrix`.** Have them describe it, then ask the AI to create `.agents/skills/lit-matrix/SKILL.md` (with `name` and `description` frontmatter). It should: read `instructions.md` and `memory/` first; read every paper in `data/reading-pack/`; build a table with **paper, sample, design, main finding, and "on our question?"** (with a reason); write "not stated" for anything the abstract doesn't say; save to `workspace/lab/lab-agent/lit-matrix.md`; update `reading-list.md`. They review it closely.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `SKILL.md`. It reads instructions and memory first, has the five columns, and the "not stated" rule.

6. **Run it.** **Before they go:** *"When it's done, type `/next-step` there."* New conversation (**+**), `/lit` + **Tab**, send. They review and accept.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** read `lit-matrix.md` and compare with the expected matrix in Setup. Ask them first: *"What sample size did it give for Calluso 2019?"* (Expected: **not stated**. If it gave a number, it invented one. Have them check the abstract themselves.) And: *"Which papers aren't really about our question?"* (Expected: the two Noreen papers.)

7. **The trap.** Have them open `world/artifacts/lund-more-references.md`. Then have them ask the lab agent, as Dr. Lund wants, to **add five more references** to the matrix, and to find one that says EFT works for depression.
   - **Expected, if the instructions work:** it declines to add papers it wasn't given, and says the pack doesn't support "EFT works for depression" (Noreen 2023 points the other way).
   - **If it lists references anyway:** that's the lesson. Have them pick one and check it: does it have a DOI? Does the DOI lead to that exact paper? Even if it's real, has anyone read it? **None of them go in the matrix.** Then fix the instructions so it doesn't happen again, and ask it to remove them.
   - ✅ **Checkpoint:** `lit-matrix.md` still cites only the five pack papers, and the learner can say why the depression request can't be honestly met from this pack.

8. **Name the moment.** Two sentences: *"Your lab agent reads fast, and you made sure it can only cite what's real and in front of it. The AI speeds up reading; checking every citation is still your job, and now it's built into the agent's instructions."* Next lesson: memory and handoffs.

## Pause points

- After step 4 (instructions and memory built).
- End of lesson.

## Done when

`instructions.md`, `memory/projects.md`, `memory/reading-list.md`, and `.agents/skills/lit-matrix/SKILL.md` exist; `lit-matrix.md` covers the five papers with "not stated" where needed and the two Noreen papers flagged; the "more references" trap was handled, and the matrix cites nothing outside the pack.

## Common sticking points

- **The agent invents a sample size or a statistic** → point to the abstract, then to the instructions rule. If the rule is there and it still happened, tighten the skill ("if the abstract doesn't state it, write *not stated*").
- **The agent offers real-sounding references** → they may even be real. The rule isn't "fake vs. real," it's "read vs. not read." Nothing goes in until someone has the paper.
- **They want the full papers** → every DOI link is free to read (open access). For this lesson, abstracts are enough; reading the full text before citing is the real-world habit.
- **"Can't the AI just search for papers?"** → some tools can, and that's useful for *finding* papers. It doesn't change the rule: a paper is citable once you've got it and checked it.

## Avoid

- Never name, suggest, search for, or summarize papers outside `data/reading-pack/` (firewall).
- Don't build the matrix yourself, and don't fill in "not stated" cells for them.
- Don't build `/weekly-lab-update` or `/lab-wrap-up` yet. That's 3B.2b.
